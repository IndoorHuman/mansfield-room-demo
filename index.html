The Study Room

A room on your own computer that reads what you have already written, and gives some of it back to you a few things at a time.

Below: what you'll see if you run it — and then a hundred years of Katherine Mansfield's days, already read.

two minutes

The whole thing, from an empty machine to a room with your own writing coming back to you. No voice — it is captioned.

Music: Erik Satie's first Gymnopédie, 1888.

what it is

what comes back

Your old writing comes back to you a few things at a time. That drip is the point, not a limitation.

what it costs

It is free, it is a second thing beside the app you already write in, and nothing you write leaves your machine.

the hours

Reading your library through once takes an evening or so — on one measured library, about five hours, running by itself.

what it is good at

Writing first. Pictures work, and they are much slower.

how far along it is

This is the first room of a house she is building.

what it cannot do yet

It only opens Mac doors. No support is promised. If you already pay for a key, it uses that one.

What it can't do

  • bringing things in is one lump — If the lid closes or it is stopped part-way, the whole thing is discarded and started again.
  • it can go quiet — It sometimes stops answering with nothing on screen to say so.
  • it does not warn you first — It starts reading without telling you how long it will take.

Named here because you may have arrived at this page on its own.

Katherine Mansfield's days

She died a hundred years ago and her writing belongs to everyone now. The room read her diary only because a person allowed it — and everything below was made in advance, on one computer, before this page existed. Nothing here is thinking while you look.

Two things you will notice. It writes to her — “you set that down” — because it always writes to whoever it is reading, and nobody told it she was gone. And some of her days come back with nothing: it will not write about a person it cannot speak to, and her quietest years are the ones it has least to say about. Those days are marked. Her own writing is on every one of them.

The room has not read her diary. It cannot see this day at all — not the words, not the date, not that it exists.

The room will hold your diary back the same way. This reflection was made in advance, not now. Pressing this changes nothing — it goes back the way it was.
