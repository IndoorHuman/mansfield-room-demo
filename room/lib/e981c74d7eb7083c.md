"When I stuck the small drawing to the side of the mirror frame I realised that the seal — the mark — the cachet rouge — had been set on the room." A drawing, a bit of gum, and the room belongs to two people instead of one — and then the inventory arrives without being asked for: "the tiny bouquet of tangerine fruits, the paste-pot on the writing-table, the fowl's feather stuck in Ribni's hair, the horn spectacles on the Chinese embroidery."

What you say about all that is not that it is prettier or fuller. It is: "The order in which I live is not changed, but enriched; in some strange way it is enlarged." The order stays. Something is added to it and it grows outward at the edges. And then, plainly, "This is en effet just the effect of his mind upon mine."

The measure you use twice over is seeing. "each other's critic in that he ' sees ' me, I see myself reflected as more than I appear and yet not more than I am" — more than appears, not more than is, the second clause doing all the careful work of the first. "So, to be together is apart from all else an act of faith in ourselves."

Then you go out into the garden and the writing changes gear entirely. The palm leaves "like down-drooping feathers," the grass "soft, unreal, like moss," and you catch yourself not certain what you are hearing: "one fancied — was it real, was it imaginary? — one heard a body of sound, one heard all the preparations for night within the houses." Charcoal broken, dishes clattered, water in the waterjugs, "Little children are sleeping...."

And the stars, which you refuse to let be dignified: "Does it always happen that while you look at the star you feel the other stars are dancing, flickering, changing places, almost playing a game on purpose to bewilder you?" You ask it as a question and answer it yourself in the next breath. I keep wondering whether that gaiety is something you find only from a cane chair with a wall behind you, or whether it would have been there just as much from the middle of the lawn.

"It is strange that there are times when I feel the stars are not at all solemn: they are secretly gay. I felt this to-night."
