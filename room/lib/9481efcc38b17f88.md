"Snow. The vegetable garden fence was all but gone." That is the whole beginning — weather, then a fence that is nearly not there, and then H arriving with a number: "between 6-7 feet of snow." He is "very cheerful and friendly," and the page says so twice over in effect, once in the words and once in the ease of the sentence.

Then the quotation. "Well, the fact is she is not normal. And anyone who is not normal I call mad. She is unconventional, that is to say, and people who are that are no good to anyone except themselves." You wrote "Off his guard" before it, which is you telling us he did not know he was being heard properly.

And you did not answer him on the page. What you gave instead was his hands: "a look came into his eyes — a flash of power — and he swung the stethoscope, then picked up my fan and rattled it open." Your fan. He picked it up while he was saying it.

The entry does not stay there. "Read and knitted and played cards." Four things in a row, no comment on any of them. Then the letter. "He does see what I meant. He does not see it as a set of trivial happenings just thrown together." That is the sentence with the most defence in it, and it is not defending you against H — it is about the story, and about being read the way you meant to be read. I keep wondering whether it took the one to make the other worth writing down, or whether the letter had already been the day's real news before he came up the path with his six or seven feet.

"This is enough to be deeply grateful for — more than others will see. But I have this continual longing to write something with all my power, all my force in it."
