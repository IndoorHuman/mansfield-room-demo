"Everything is in a state of suspense — even birds and chimneys."

The page begins in waiting and ends in a room with other people in it, and between those two there is a walk: "At the last moment L.M. never said Good-bye at all but took the fiddle and ran. I walked away down some narrow streets; large drops of rain fell."

What stays with me is that nothing is resolved and the smell arrives anyway — "the delicious smell of fresh wood and straw reminded me of Wellington. I could almost fancy a sawmill." Suspense is not lifted; it is simply walked through, past packing warehouses, into rain.

And then the last line does not explain itself either: "In the evening the C.'s, and the little parrot swinging on a wire." No account of the evening, no verb even — just the company and the parrot, kept the way you keep a thing you saw. I wonder whether it is the parrot or the wire that made that sentence worth setting down.
