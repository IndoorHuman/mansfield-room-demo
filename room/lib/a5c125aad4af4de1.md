"It's very quiet. I've re-read UEntrave."

That is how the November page starts, and it does not stay quiet for long — it goes straight over to Colette. "I suppose Colette is the only woman in France who does just this. I don't care a fig at present for anyone I know except her."

A fig is the smallest coin of not-caring there is, and you spend it on everyone you know, at present, with the qualifier tucked in like a hand kept free.

Then the turn: "But the book to be written is still unwritten. I can't sit down and fire away like J."

So the page holds three things in a row — the quiet, the re-reading, the unwritten book — and only the last one gets a "But" in front of it. I keep wondering what "does just this" meant in your ear as you wrote it, whether it was a way of putting sentences down or a way of going about a day.

"It's very quiet. I've re-read UEntrave."
