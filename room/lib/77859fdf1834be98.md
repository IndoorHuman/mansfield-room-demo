"A sudden idea of the relationship between ' lovers.'" It is announced as sudden before it has said anything at all, and then what follows is not sudden in the least — it is patient, worked through in order. "We are neither male nor female. We are a compound of both." The choosing, the expanding, and then "Being made whole." set down in quotation marks as though borrowed from somewhere, and immediately taken up again: "Yes, but that's a process."

The reason for one rather than many turns out not to be love at all. "And why I choose one man for this rather than many is for safety." Then the ring, and the wall, and "Here is safety for us to grow."

And then the whole thing gets its hand pulled back: "Why, I talk like a child". I keep wondering whether the wall or the growing came into your head first — whether the ring was drawn to keep something out, or drawn round something already sprouting.
