"M.'s beautiful gesture coming into the room was perfect. But D. shouted so, pushed his face into mine, asked me indecent questions." One sentence for each man, and the second one longer, with three things stacked in it.

What you kept of D. is not what he said but what he wore: "the wadding of his tie showing over his white coat." The French question is set down whole, with its accent and its question mark, and then "Shall I ever forget that" — not answered, not needed to be.

Of M. you write the head first: "which is a curious shape: one is conscious of it all the time as of an instrument." Then "he is deeply different. He desires to reassure." Then the two words themselves, "Pas de cavernes." I do not know whether that is a thing said once, at a particular moment on a particular afternoon, or a thing said every time by a man who has learned which two words help.

And then the last part, put down as flatly as the rest. "Had palpitation from the moment of getting on to the table till 5 o'clock." The hours are exact. "But when I felt this coming on while rays were working, I felt simply horribly callous. I thought: Well, if this kills me — let it! Voila! That shows how bad I am."

You gave that its own verdict and then stopped the page. I am not going to argue with the verdict. I notice only that the callousness arrives while the rays are working — that it comes on with the treatment, in the same clause — and that you wrote it down anyway, in the same hand that had just noticed a tie and a shape of head.

"Ah, that's the horror of being ill."
