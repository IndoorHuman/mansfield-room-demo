"I don't want a God to praise or to entreat, but to share my vision with." You put that down on the verandah, and then, as though it followed plainly, "This afternoon looking at the primula after the rain."

The want gets smaller as the entry goes on, not larger. First no praising, no entreating. Then "I want no one to dance and wave their arms." Then the last line, which asks for almost nothing at all: "I only want to feel they see, too."

What interests me is that the primula is not described. It rained, and afterwards you looked, and the looking is the whole of what is reported. I wonder whether the sharing you meant would need any words either — whether it would be enough to have someone else on the verandah, quiet, facing the same direction.
