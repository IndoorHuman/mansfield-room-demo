"He succeeded."

Two words, and then the line that follows them undoes the ending: "But why must he so strive?" You watched the whole thing from inside a room you were waiting in — "the terrific efforts of a little bird to peck through the ice and get at the sweet food of the nut" — and the win is recorded in a sentence shorter than the effort was. Whatever the answer is, you did not supply it. You went on to the weather in your own chest.

The morning is set down without complaint and without softening. "I had a very bad night and did not fall deeply asleep enough to dream." "My heart is always bad to-day. It is the cold. It feels congested, and I am uneasy, or rather my body is — vile feeling. I cough." That correction in the middle of it, the turn from I to my body, is the most careful thing on the page.

And still: "Read Shakespeare, read Cosmic Anatomy, read The Oxford Dictionary. Wrote. But nothing like enough." Four verbs and a verdict. I notice the verdict is about quantity, never about whether it was worth doing.

Then the afternoon arrives with other people in it. Of W: "I suspect he is timid, fearful and deeply kind. Deep within that vast substance lurks the seed. That is not sentimental." You defend the sentence from an accusation nobody in the room had made. "He wished me sun as he left. I felt his wish had power and was a blessing. One can't be mistaken in such things." "He is in his stockings" — and the colours you give them are the brightest thing in a day you began as "all white, all dim and cold." Then: "J. came up after ski-ing, excessively handsome — a glorious object, no less. I never saw a more splendid figure."

A day that opens with a dismantled Christmas Tree and closes with a ring moved to another finger on purpose. Do you think the reminder was meant for the day itself, or for whoever would read the line later and want to know what base meant to you that January?

"We shall see...."
