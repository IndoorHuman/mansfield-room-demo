"A misty, misty evening. I want to write down the fact that not only am I not afraid of death — I welcome the idea of death. I believe in immortality because he is not here, and I long to join him." You set that down without preamble, and then the very next line turns it into an errand: "First, my darling, I've got things to do for both of us, and then I will come as quickly as I can."

The things to do are named soon after. "Because I feel I have a duty to perform to the lovely time when we were both alive. I want to write about it, and he wanted me to." It sits in the same page as "I am just as much dead as he is" and does not argue with it. Both stay.

And in the middle of that page a woman is singing somewhere in the corridor, and out of the song comes a house — "flower patches under the windows and the soft mass of a haystack at the back," the fowls "woolly blurs on the perches," the pony with a cloth on, the man "still young and careless" who walks quicker when the light comes on in the window. Then, straight after: "But where are these comely people?"

By January the errand has a shape. "Now — now I want to write recollections of my own country. Yes, I want to write about my own country till I simply exhaust my store." You allow yourself the whole of it — "I shall tell everything, even of how the laundry-basket squeaked at 75" — and then set the limit yourself: "No novels, no problem stories, nothing that is not simple, open." In between there is "I feel always trembling on the brink of poetry."

February answers with furniture. "I have put a table to-day in my room, facing a corner, but from where I sit I can see some top shoots of the almond-tree and the sea sounds loud." The doubt is still there in the same entry — "Oh, yes, we've heard that before! " — but the table is there too, and it faces a corner, which I keep turning over. Was the corner so that nothing could interrupt, or so that the almond-tree had to be looked for rather than simply given?

"There is a vase of beautiful geraniums on the table. Nothing could be nicer than this spot, and it's so quiet and so high, like sitting up in a tree."
