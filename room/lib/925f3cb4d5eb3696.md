# Katherine Mansfield — a life

> **Note on sources.** This is an *authored* reference note, assembled from
> public biographical fact so an assistant reading this vault has the outline
> of her life. The quotations are verbatim from the public-domain 1927
> *Journal* and 1929 *Letters* in this vault; everything else is summary.

**Kathleen Mansfield Beauchamp**, born 14 October 1888 in Wellington, New
Zealand; died 9 January 1923 at Fontainebleau, France, aged 34.

## The shape of it

- **Wellington, 1888–1903.** Third daughter of Harold Beauchamp, a banker who
  became chairman of the Bank of New Zealand, and Annie Burnell Dyer. Childhood
  at Thorndon and then at **Karori** — the house and garden that become the
  Burnells' in "Prelude," "At the Bay," and "The Doll's House."
- **London, 1903–1906.** Queen's College, Harley Street. She discovered she
  wanted to be a writer and did not want to go home.
- **Back to New Zealand, 1906–1908.** Restless, thwarted, playing the cello.
  A camping trip through the Urewera country gave her the first notebook that
  reads like her.
- **London again, 1908.** She never returned to New Zealand. A brief disastrous
  marriage to George Bowden (left the same day), a pregnancy, a miscarriage in
  Bavaria — which produced *In a German Pension* (1911), the book she later
  refused to have reprinted: *"It is far too immature, and I don't even
  acknowledge it to-day."*
- **John Middleton Murry, from 1911.** Critic and editor; her partner, sparring
  partner, first reader, and eventually her husband (1918). He edited both the
  *Journal* and the *Letters* in this vault after her death.
- **Leslie, 1915.** Her adored younger brother arrived in England as an officer,
  they talked for hours about their childhood, and he was killed in France a
  month later. His death turned her back toward New Zealand as her subject.
- **Tuberculosis, from 1917.** The rest of her life is a map of places she went
  to be less ill: Bandol, Menton, San Remo, Montana, Sierre, Fontainebleau. She
  wrote her best work in it.
- **The great work, 1920–1922.** *Bliss* (1920) and *The Garden Party* (1922) —
  the books that made her. She died the following January.

## The people she wrote to

Her correspondents in this vault are [[people]]. The centre of gravity is
**J. M. Murry**; the warmest letters are to his younger brother **Richard**,
a painter she wrote to about craft as if to a colleague.

## Why this vault exists

Her life ran on memory. Cut off from New Zealand, then cut off by illness from
almost everything else, she wrote by going back — deliberately, as work. That
is the whole thesis of the room this vault is a demonstration for.
