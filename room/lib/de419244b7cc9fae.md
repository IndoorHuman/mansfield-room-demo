"Another hellish day. But J. found some pastilles which help my throat, and it seemed to me they had a calming effect on my heart." The sentence turns on that But, and it turns fast — the day is named once and then the pastilles get the rest of the line, and the heart gets the end of it.

Then, with nothing in between: "I had one of my perfect dreams. I was at sea, sailing with my parasol opened to just a ' freshet ' of wind. Heavenly the sea, the sky, the land — parasol pink — boat pale pink."

Two pinks, and the second one lighter. You did not say which came first in the dream, the parasol or the boat, or whether the paleness was the water taking some of the colour off.

The page does not stay there. "If I could only get over my discouragement! But who is going to help with that!" Both of those end in exclamation and neither is answered. What follows is the count of what has to be done and what it costs — "The effort uses what remains of my strength. By 5 o'clock I am finished and must go to bed again." That is stated the way you state a fact about the weather, and in fact the weather comes next.

"It is a very dull day. The canaries sing." You put those side by side without joining them.

And at the bottom, Bunin: "He is not a sympathetic soul, but it is good to read him... he carries one away." The same shape as the pastilles at the top — something that is not on your side exactly, but does something for you anyway. Carried away, and at sea, and pink.
