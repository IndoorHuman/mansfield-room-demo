"I shall not remember what happened on this day. It is a blank." You wrote that on the day itself, which is the strange part — not looking back at a gap but standing inside one and marking it while it was still going on.

Then the accounting: "At the end of my life I may want it, may long to have it." And then, against all of it, one thing held back from the blank: "There was a new moon: that I remember."

A new moon is the one you cannot see. That is what survived the day — the dark shape of it, the thinnest possible fact.

"But who came or what I did — all is lost."

And still it gets a page, and a date at the top. I keep turning over what made it worth the ink: whether writing down that nothing stayed is its own way of keeping the day, or whether the moon was always the point and the rest was just how you got to it.

"It's just a day missed, a day crossing the line."
