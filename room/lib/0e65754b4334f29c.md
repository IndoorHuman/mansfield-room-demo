"Went to the Palace, and had the gland punctured. It is very unlikely that they will save the skin." You set that down in the tone of an errand run, and then you go further rather than back: "I am sure, from the feeling, that they won't, and that this affair is only beginning." Nothing is put around it. The sentence after is "In the meantime I am exhausted and can't write a stroke."

And then a heading — A Grey Day — as though the page had been allowed to start over.

What follows is an argument you hold on both sides at once. "I thought I could, but I felt tired after tea and rested instead. Is it good or bad in me to behave so?" And a few lines down, without settling it: "I have a sense of guilt, but at the same time I know that to rest is the very best thing I can do." The stories are standing about outside — "Look at the stories that wait and wait just at the threshold. Why don't I let them in?" — and others behind them, "lurking beyond just out there — waiting for the chance."

Next morning you take the whole thing back. "I don't want to write anything. It's grey; it's heavy and dull." Then: "I don't want to write; I want to live. What does she mean by that? It's not easy to say. But there you are!" You put yourself in the third person to ask it, which is a way of standing a little to the side of your own question.

The method turns up plainly: "if I go on long enough, I break through. Yes, it's rather like tossing very large flat stones into the stream." And directly after, the doubt about the method — "The question is, though, how long this will prove efficacious."

Then the day itself gets in. "And yet I love this quiet clouded day. A bell sounds from afar; the birds sing one after another as if they called across the tree-tops. I love this settled stillness, and this feeling that, at any moment, down may come the rain." The sky "silvery white, streaked with little clouds." And the flies, which get the flattest line of all: "there is nothing really to be done for them: I feel that about hardly anything."

In the middle of it, arriving from nowhere and left unexplained: "I suddenly found myself outside the library in Wcerishofen: spring — lilac — rain — books in black bindings." I keep wondering whether it was the smell of the rain coming that fetched it, or the black bindings that came first and the rain afterwards.

And the eye goes on working regardless — the barmaid with "an immense amount of fuzzy hair piled up on top of her head, and several very large rings," the rings you felt certain were engagement-rings; the mutton chops, "such an ill-bred smell."

"Should you say wasted? No, not really. Something is gathered. This quiet time brings one nearer."
