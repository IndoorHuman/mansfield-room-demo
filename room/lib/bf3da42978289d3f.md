"The heavens opened for the sunset to-night."

That comes first, before the window, before the rain, before any of the thinking. Then: "When I had thought the day folded and sealed, came a burst of heavenly bright petals...." The day had already been decided against, and something arrived anyway.

What happens after is a question you put down and did not answer: "Now who is to decide between 'Let it be' and 'Force it'?" J. gets the whip and the idle steed. You get the other side of it — "if mine does not gallop and dance at free will, I am not riding at all, but just swinging from its tail." It is the image of a rider who is not steering, only holding on, and the page does not treat that as a failure so much as a description.

And then the last three words settle it for the evening without settling the argument at all: "To-night he's all sparks."

I keep thinking about the order — the sky first, the hard thing melting, and only then the question about method. Does the argument only come up on the nights when it has already been answered by weather?
