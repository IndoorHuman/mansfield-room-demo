"I positively feel, in my hideous modern way, that I can't get into touch with my mind." And then the whole thing turns into a telephone box — "I am standing gasping in one of those disgusting telephone boxes and I can't ' get through.'" — with an operator in it, and an exchange, and a voice that tinkles: " Sorry. There's no reply."

What holds me is that she keeps asking anyway. " Will you ring them again — Exchange? A good long ring. There must be somebody there." The insistence is written out in full, and then answered flatly: " I can't get any answer."

The building is described in the order things arrive to her: "it's dark and empty and quiet... above all — empty." The last word is the one she goes back for.

Then the note underneath, which is not part of the metaphor at all and is the truest line in it: "A queer thing is that I keep seeing it — this empty building — as my father's office. I smell it as that. I see the cage of the clumsy wooden goods lift and the tarred ropes hanging." The emptiness has furniture. It has a smell. Whatever she could not get through to, she could see the lift cage in it clearly enough to write it down — and I wonder whether the seeing was the getting through, or something else that came instead.
