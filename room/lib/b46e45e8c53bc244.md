"Spent the evening writing another column. Help me, God!" — and then the interruption comes from outside the sentence: "And then L. M. came in to say I was half-an-hour slow. Just did it in time."

The evening keeps going after that. "Had talk with L.M. Our friendship is returning — in the old fashion." You put the old fashion in without explaining what the old fashion was, as though it were a thing anyone would recognise.

Then "Thought out ' The Exile.'" Then: "Appalling night of misery."

Four sentences of work and talk, and the last line stands by itself with nothing attached to it, no because, no and so. I keep looking at the order — the column, the clock, the friendship returning, the story thought out, and then that. I wonder whether the order is the order they happened in, or whether the misery had been running underneath all of it and only got written down when there was nothing left to write down.
