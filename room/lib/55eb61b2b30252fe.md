"Oh, to be a writer, a real writer given up to it and to it alone!" — and then, in the same breath, the turning back: "I failed to-day; I turned back, looked over my shoulder, and immediately it happened, I felt as though I too were struck down."

What comes after that is not an explanation but a whole London evening: "the clang of the gates as they close the garden," "the smell of leaves and dust," "the languor of twilight, the breath of it on one's cheek." All of it named precisely, all of it filed under "those things which (I feel to-day) are gone from me for ever...." And then the plain sentence, set down without softening: "I feel today that I shall die soon and suddenly: but not of my lungs." I am not going to move it or put anything on top of it.

The page does not stay there, though. Dickens arrives, "carried away. That is bliss." And the roosters: "By night and at early morning I love to listen to my darling roosters crowing to one another from lonely yards. Each one has a different note: I have never heard two roosters crow alike."

How long had you been listening, to be that certain no two of them are the same?
