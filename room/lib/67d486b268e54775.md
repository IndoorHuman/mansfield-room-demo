# Memory MOC

If you only read one hub, read this one — it is the reason this vault is the
demo for the Study Room rather than any other corpus.

Mansfield lost access to her own life twice: she left New Zealand at nineteen
and never went back, and from 1917 tuberculosis took most of the rest. What she
did about it was **go back on purpose, as work.** The stories everyone
remembers her for are all acts of retrieval.

## She says it plainly
> "I remember everything, and perhaps the great joy of Life to me is in playing
> just that game."

## And she is honest that it costs
> "It brings nothing but memories — and by memories I mean those that one cannot
> without pain remember."

That pair is the whole design problem this app exists for. Resurfacing is a joy
and a risk in the same motion — which is why the room asks before it offers, and
why it never counts your days.

## What sets it off
> "Ah, Jeanne, anyone who says to me, 'Do you remember?' simply has my heart..."

## And what it actually looks like
> "It is strange to remember buying a copy of The Native Companion on Lambton
> Quay and standing under a lamppost with darling Leslie to see if my story had
> been printed."

> "The damson trees are the first I remember seeing since those at Karori."

Leslie is her brother, killed in France in 1915. Small concrete objects — a
magazine, a lamppost, a damson tree — carrying the whole weight. That is
exactly how the shelf is supposed to work.
