# Letters MOC

112 letters, 1920–1922, from *The Letters of Katherine Mansfield*, Volume Two
(1929), edited by J. M. Murry. Verbatim public-domain text.

## Who she wrote to, and how
- **[[people|J. M. Murry]]** (14) — her husband. Domestic, exacting, unguarded;
  money, proofs, and what she thought of his work.
- **[[people|Richard Murry]]** (14) — his younger brother, a painter. The
  best letters in the book. Craft talk between equals, no performance.
- **[[people|Anne Estelle Rice]]** (10) — painter friend. Warm and funny.
- **[[people|Lady Ottoline Morrell]]** (8) — Garsington. More performed.
- **[[people|William Gerhardi]]** (8) — young novelist she encouraged.
- **[[people|Sir Harold Beauchamp]]** (6) — her father. Formal, dutiful, and
  quietly angling for approval.
- **[[people|S. S. Koteliansky]]** (6), the **Schiffs** (15 across three
  addressings), **Sylvia Lynd** (4), and others.

## The through-line
These are the years of Menton, Montana, and Sierre — every letter written from
somewhere she had gone to be less ill. The astonishing thing is how little
they complain and how much they notice.
