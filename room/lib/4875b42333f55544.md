"Went to London. We found B.C. had arrived; so D. put us up." The whole day arrives in that register — arrivals, someone putting someone up, tea at the Criterion, "Had my hands done." Nothing is explained, and the initials stand in for people the page assumes you already know.

Then the evening: "went to the Oxford and saw Marie Lloyd, who was very good." Four words of verdict and no more. "Slept on the big divan in A.'s room."

And the last sentence turns the day back on itself, out of order: "In the afternoon it was very foggy in London; but the relief to be there was immense." The afternoon comes after the evening, as though it had to be said and there was no earlier place left for it. I keep wondering whether the fog belongs to the relief or is only what the relief had to be felt through.
