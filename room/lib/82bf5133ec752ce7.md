"Work." A single word, and then the whole rest of it comes pouring after: "my love of work — my desire to be a better writer — my longing to take greater pains." You let the dashes do the carrying there, one thing pulling the next out behind it, and you do not stop to justify any of it.

Then the accounting of what it stands in for. "It takes the place of religion — it is my religion — of people — I create my people: of ' life ' — it is Life." The quotation marks you put round ' life ' and not round Life are the smallest thing on the page and they do the most.

And then the temptation, named plainly: to kneel, to adore, to stay too long. "I must be more busy about my master's business."

After that the page changes weather entirely. "The shape of every flower is like a sound." It is a sentence that does not argue with itself. Neither does the fly in the magnolia, which is only there, having dropped in by mistake.

What I keep turning over is the last turn — the chariot of fire, the uncertainty about whether it was Isaiah or Elisha left standing in its brackets, and then: "But when the weather is divine and I am free to work, such a journey is positively nothing." You set the ascension beside a good working day and the good working day wins. Did that comparison arrive as a joke, or did you mean it exactly as flatly as it reads?
