"B's letter was the most beautiful I have ever received. It gave me a strange shock to find J. never even asked how things were going." The two letters arrive in the same sentence-pair and only one of them is described. The other gets its measure taken by what is missing from it: "absolutely impersonal. It might have been written to anyone."

Then the excusing begins, and stops itself. "True, he was anxious for the post. But... that was because he is alone." You give the reason and take it back inside four words and an ellipsis, and what follows is a question you leave standing: "Do I make J. up?"

After that the page turns hard and level. "Then let it continue so. But I will not take a house anywhere. I, too, will be free." And then, in brackets, as though it needed permission from no one but was going to be entered into the record anyway: "(I write exactly as I feel.)"

The day's second half does not pretend to be a different day. "A bad day. I feel ill, in an obscure way — horrible pains and so on, and weakness. I could do nothing. The weakness was not only physical." You do not soften that, and I am not going to. What you put next to it is not comfort either, it is a plan: "I must heal my Self before I will be well. Yes, that is the important thing. No attention is needed here. This must be done alone and at once."

I keep returning to the parenthesis. Everything else on this page is stated straight out — the shock, the relief in every line, the fish that has escaped from the hook — and only the sentence about your own honesty gets put in brackets. I wonder who the brackets were for.
