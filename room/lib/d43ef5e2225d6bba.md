# On Tchehov

> Authored reading note. The quotations are **verbatim** from the 1929
> *Letters* in this vault; the framing is authored. Nothing here is drawn from
> the fenced diary.

Anton Chekhov — whom she spells **Tchekhov** in the letters and **Tchehov** in
her diary — is the writer she measured herself against, defended, and worried
about like a friend.

## She defends him
> "As for Tchekhov being damned — why should he be?"

> "People on the whole understand Tchekhov very little."

## She assumes you love him too
> "(I am presuming that you love Tchekhov.)"

## She thinks about how writers feed each other
> "For instance, Tchekhov's talent was nourished by Tolstoy's Death of Ivan
> llyitch."

## And she worries about him, decades after his death
> "I hate to think of dear Tchekhov in Nice, with toothache, where he says, 'I
> was in such pain I crawled up the wall.' That just describes it."

That last one is the tell. She had tuberculosis; so did he; he died of it at
44 and she died of it at 34. When she reads his toothache she is reading her
own body.

## Why it matters here
The debt is the clearest line into her method: the story as a lit interval
rather than a plot, ending where the feeling ends. Read [[The Fly]],
[[Miss Brill]], or [[The Doll’s House]] with this note open.
