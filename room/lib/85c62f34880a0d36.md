"Wrote and finished Taking the Veil. It took me about 3 hours to write finally. But I had been thinking over the decor and so on for weeks — nay, months, I believe." The three hours get counted and the months only get guessed at, and the guess is where you stop to correct yourself mid-sentence.

Then the place: "I suppose I have not been in the grounds more than twice. But it is one of the places that remains as vivid as ever." Twice is enough, apparently, and you say so without arguing for it. Writing about the convent "seemed so natural" — no explanation offered for why so little should hold so well.

The page closes on two names and an instruction to yourself: "I must not forget the name of Miss Sparrow,1 nor the name Palmer." Not the convent, not Wellington, not the ranging about — names. I wonder what a name has to be carrying, for it to be the thing worth setting down last.
