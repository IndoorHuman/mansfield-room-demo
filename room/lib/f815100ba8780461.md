"I feel a bit more cheerful to-day because I don't look quite so revolting as I have done." That is how the second of February starts, and it does not hold. "No, the day ended in being as bad as ever. For one thing my illness is really severe." The correction comes in the same breath as the cheer, in the same hand, and you did not go back and soften either one.

Then: "I have been embroidering my kimono with black wool. Bah! What rot! What do I care for such rubbish!" The work still happened. You sat with the black wool and got some distance into it, and then wrote it off. I notice you told me what the wool was and what the garment was before you told me it was rubbish.

The day before had its own turn in it. "A slight attack of 'flu' is bowling me over. There is a glimpse of sun. The trees look as though they were hanging out to dry." The trees get the best line of both days and they get it while you are being bowled over. And at the very end, after "Life was like sawdust and sand" — "Talked short stories to J." No adjective on it. Just the fact that it happened.

I keep wondering whether the trees hanging out to dry came to you at the window or came later, when you were writing it down.
