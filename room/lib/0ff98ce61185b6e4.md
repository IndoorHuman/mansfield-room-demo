"So he went into his bedroom and shut the door between, and I sat on the end of the sofa." The door shuts and the room begins — that is the order of it. She is left alone with the sun coming full through the two windows, "dividing the studio into four — two quarters of light and two of shadow," and everything the light touches "seemed to float in it, to bathe and to sparkle in it as if they belonged not to land, but to water."

And then the doubt, which she does not resolve: "it is only the clear, trembling water that dances — but — can you be quite sure?... No, not quite sure, and that little Chinese group on the writing table may or may not have shaken itself awake for just one hundredth of a second out of hundreds of years of sleep."

After that the room is only listed. A blue tea-pot with two white cups attending. "a red apple among oranges addeth fire to flame." The books flying up and down in scales of colour, "with pink and lilac notes recurring." Two sticks and an umbrella in one corner. In the fireplace, "a kettle, curiously like a bird."

The two lines I keep setting side by side are these: "White net curtains hang over the windows. For all the sun it is raining outside." Sun through both windows and rain outside them, held in one breath, no explanation offered. Is that the thing you were after — that a room can be sunlit and raining at once and neither cancels the other?

And through all of it, someone whistling in the next room while he dresses.

"Reposez-vous.

Oui, je me repose...."
