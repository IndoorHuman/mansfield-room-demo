"This is simply the most Divine Spot. So remote, so peaceful; full of colour, full of Autumn; the sunset is real and the sound of somebody splitting small wood is real, too." Twice in one line you say real, and both times about something you cannot see doing itself — the sunset, and somebody out of sight with an axe. Then the wish that follows: "If only one could live up here for really a long time and not have to see anybody...." People at a distance, then, but their noise permitted. The wood being split is company enough.

Below that the page turns to other rooms entirely — a door "that can't bear people trying to burst their way in and loathes people trying to burst their way out," a porter's cave, "a big tea-stained china tea cup crowned with its saucer." And then, further down, "Again there came that silence that was a question".

You wrote the remote place and the loud reluctant door on the same page, and I keep wondering which one the good-night belongs to.
