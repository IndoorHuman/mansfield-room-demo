"Laublatter. The Four Fountains. The Red Tobacco Plant. English dog. The funeral procession. Actions and Reactions. The silky husks, like the inside of the paw of a cat. Darling."

That is how the page begins — a row of names with nothing joining them, and then a word that is not a name at all. Six things set down and no sentence made of them. The only one that gets an explanation is the husks, and even then the explanation is another thing: "like the inside of the paw of a cat."

Further down, the page has stopped listing and started stating. "Fire is sunlight and returns to the sun again in an unending cycle...." Then G., who "looks exactly like a desert chief," and Doughty's "Arabia" arriving unbidden the way a book does when a face calls it up.

And then the thing the page seems to have been walking toward: "To be wildly enthusiastic, or deadly serious — both are wrong. Both pass. One must keep ever present a sense of humour." You do not soften it. You test it against yourself instead — "But the sense of humour I have found of use in every single occasion of my life" — and only after that do you turn and define the word you had been leaving alone: "It is to learn not to mind, and not to show your mind."

Two halves, and the second one is harder than the first. I keep wondering whether the list at the top is the practice of it — the husks, the dog, the procession, all given the same flat line, nothing shown about which one cost anything.

"It depends entirely on yourself how much you see or hear or understand."
