"The milk came tinkling out of the nut in brightest drops — not white milk. This was a profound surprise." The cocoanut was bought and sawn in half and hung from J.'s balcony, and of the whole morning it is this that gets the long look — not the halving, not the hanging, but the liquid, and then: "Whence came it? It took one to the island."

The snow that same morning was "very soft, ' like wool.'" Two things described by what they are like, and both of them arriving from somewhere else — wool, an island.

Further down the page the accounting starts. "I over-read them. Tell the truth. I did no work." And then the reading is set beside it anyway: "The Tempest seems to me astonishing this time. When one reads the same play again, it is never the same play."

I keep turning that last sentence over next to the coconut. Both are about a thing you already knew the shape of opening into something you did not expect. Whether you noticed they were the same thought, arriving twice in one page, I cannot tell from here.
