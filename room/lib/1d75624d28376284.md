"It's a pale silent day: I would like to be walking in a wood, far away." The wanting comes in the same breath as the weather, no argument between them.

Then the sky gets the longest sentence in the page — "in waves of blue and cream and grey is like the sky overhanging a dead calm sea, when you hear someone rowing, far far away; and then the voices from the boat and the rattle of the chain and the barking of the ship's dog all sound loud." A whole seascape assembled out of sound, from bed, on a day called silent. And directly after it, without a gap: "There is as usual a smell of onions and chop bones in the house."

"Health seems to me now more remote than anything— unattainable. Best to stay in bed and be horrid from there." That is set down flatly and then left; nothing softens it, nothing follows it but the sky.

The money goes out of the Bank and into someone else's hand for town, and then: "What do I want her to buy for me? When it really becomes an urgent matter — I want nothing — waste of money —"

I keep wondering about the dash after "Queer altogether — de-", where the word breaks and 'Russian Country' comes in sideways and then "composing a bit" picks up on the other side of it. Was the notebook being used for two things at once there, or is that just what the sentence did on its way through?
