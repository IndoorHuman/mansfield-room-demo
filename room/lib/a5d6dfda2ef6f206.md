"I really only ask for time to write it all — time to write my books. Then I don't mind dying. I live to write." Six o'clock, your own room, and a page or two before that you had written "I am sitting in my own room thinking of Mother: I want to cry." You did not soften the one with the other. They sit in the order you put them, and after them comes "The lovely world (God, how lovely the external world is!) is there and I bathe in it and am refreshed."

The notebook is uncertain of itself from the first line: "I wish I had some idea of how old this note book is. The writing is very faint and far away." And then, over a page headed with one year, "Now it is May 1919."

The going out and the coming back is all here, small and exact. The wind at the corner — "It was too much to bear." The Yale key "like a thief," the door "dead quiet," and then the stairs, and then the blouse-pattern. "Purple chiffon front neck sleeves." "Am I interrupting your work?" — "Yes, we'll call it that." You asked it plainly on the page and did not answer it: "What is there in all this to make me hate her so?"

And then Saturday, and a different weather altogether. "This joy of being alone. What is it? I feel so gay and at peace — the whole house takes the air." A baked egg, apricots and cream, cheese straws and black coffee. "A baby meal!"

You wrote that Saturday twice. In the first, "Mother shares it with me." In the second she is not there, and instead: "There is nobody in the house, and yet whose is this faint whispering? On the stairs there are tiny spots of gold — tiny footprints...." The cat's paw under the sofa frill becomes, on the second pass, "the game of the Darting Paw." I keep wondering whether the whispering on the stairs is where she went, or whether it is only that the second telling wanted the house empty.

The geraniums get the same treatment — shouted at first over a whole paragraph, then cut to the bone. Either way you leave yourself standing in it: "the little Colonial walking in the London garden patch — allowed to look, perhaps, but not to linger." And the Tinakori hills come up inside the geraniums' own jeering, which is a strange place to keep them.
