"The truth is one can get only so much into a story; there is always a sacrifice." You set that down as though it closed the question, and then immediately opened it again: "One has to leave out what one knows and longs to use. Why? I haven't any idea, but there it is."

The page turns on itself twice. "But time is not really in it." And then, straight after, "Yet wait. I do not understand even now. I am pursued by time myself."

The one stretch of leisure you name does not stay leisure by the end of its own sentence: "The only occasion when I ever felt at leisure was while writing The Daughters of the Late Colonel. And at the end I was so terribly unhappy that I wrote as fast as possible for fear of dying before the story was sent."

And still: "I should like to prove this, to work at real leisure. Only thus can it be done."

When you wrote "It's always a kind of race to get in as much as one can before it disappears" — which is the thing that disappears, the story, or the seeing of it?
