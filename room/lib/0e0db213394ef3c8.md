"Went to the fish museum at Monaco. Must remember the bubbles as the man plunged the rod into the tanks." The instruction comes first — must remember — and it is for the bubbles, not the fish, not the man, not the museum. Then, with no bridge at all: "The young girl. How naice! Young girls make me feel forty."

The entry moves the way a glance moves. Three words for the girl, then the mimicry of her voice, then what she does to you, and then the correction you make to yourself before anyone can make it for you: "Well, one certainly doesn't want to look 21."

After that it stops mid-thought. "The woman with her three little children at Monte...." — the four dots do the rest, and whatever was going to be said about her stays at Monte.

I keep wondering whether the bubbles were meant to be remembered for their own sake or for a page somewhere else, and whether the woman with her three little children was on her way to becoming the same kind of note.
