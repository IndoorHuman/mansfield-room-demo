"Determined to review two books to-day and to get on with \" Second Helping.\"" — that is how it begins, with the day's work named and counted, and then the doctor comes in and the sentences break into singing: "Diddle-dum-dum-dee! Cod is the only word! Bad-in-age! Flat-ter-ie! Gal-ant-ter-ie! Frogs!!!" The syllables get hyphens as though someone were mocking them out loud, one beat at a time.

Then "Vous pouvez vous promener. Liar. The palm tree." Three things with nothing joining them, and no explanation offered for the palm tree at all.

The review does not get finished. "Did not finish review; but no matter, it goes." You let yourself off, in the same breath as the letting off.

And under all of it, on its own line, with nothing after it: "Anguish".

I keep looking at the space between the last hyphenated word and that one. Whether the singing came before it or was already made of it, the page does not say.
