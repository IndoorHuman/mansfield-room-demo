"My room is horrible. Very noisy: a constant clatter and a feeling as though it were doorless." The room is the first thing, before the illness, before the food, before the night — and it is the doorlessness you settle on, not the clatter itself. A room with a door would hold the same noise differently.

Then the plainness of "Stayed in bed; felt very ill, but don't mind because of the reason." You do not write down the reason. You write that it is one, and that it makes the illness bearable, and you move on to the food, which was "really appalling: nothing to eat."

And the last line stands by itself with nothing softening it: "At night old Casetta feelings, like madness. Voices and words and halfvisions." You call them old, and you name where they come from, as though they were something already known and already named once before. I keep looking at "halfvisions" written closed up like that, one word.
