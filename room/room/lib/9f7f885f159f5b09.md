"Little round birds in the fir-tree at the side window, scouring the tree for food." You crumbled bread for them and only two found it, and that is set down without complaint, the way you set down the weather. "There was a strange remoteness in the air, the scene, the winter cheeping."

Then the turn, in the same short paragraph: "In the evening, for the first time for 1 felt rested. I sat up in bed and discovered I was singing within." Discovered — not decided, not managed. And immediately the world adjusts to it: "Even the sound of the wind is different. It is joyful, not ominous and black. Dark looks in at the window and is only black dark." I keep going back to that last one. Dark that is only itself, with nothing extra in it.

The rain arrives after, "long glancing rain, falling aslant," and then the page changes register entirely: "I have not done the work I should have done." "This is very bad. In fact I am disgusted with myself. There must be a change from now on." That sits on the same day as the singing, and I am not going to smooth it over — you put both there.

What follows the reproach is not more reproach, though. It is Jane Austen. "What I chiefly admire in Jane Austen is that what she promises, she performs, i.e. if Sir T. is to arrive, we have his arrival at length, and it's excellent and exceeds our expectations." You go straight from disgust to admiration, and the admiration is specific — arrival at length, exceeding expectation. Even naming your "very weakest point," you are naming what you love in a book.

I wonder whether the wind sounded different because of the rest, or whether you only heard the rest once the wind told you.

"I sat up in bed and discovered I was singing within."
