"I went out and bought some milk and honey and Veda bread." Everything in the August page comes after that, and everything in it seems to have been made possible by it — the tea, the talk, the sitting down. Then: "I feel so honest with him that it's simply one of my real joys, one of the real joys of my life." The sentence says joy twice, as if once would not hold it.

"There is a division: people who are my people, people who are not my people. He is mine."

And then, having said the largest thing on the page, she gives the brass frog away and goes out and looks up: "When we walked out I saw the sky again after all the day's blindness — little cloud and big clouds." Then "That is all. But I wanted to make a note of it."

What follows is a different voice entirely, and I like that the same page holds both. The four lines standing on their own — "They meet and just touch. They come together and part. They are separated and meet again. They realise their tie." Then the branch: "It is astonishing how violently a big branch shakes when a silly little bird has left it." Then the small hard verdict on Forster, which is one of the funniest things in the pool: "Feel this teapot. Is it not beautifully warm? Yes, but there ain't going to be no tea."

And under Living Alone, written twice as a heading before anything is said: "Even if I should, by some awful chance, find a hair upon my bread and honey — at any rate it is my own hair."

The rhubarb gets snipped, the gas gets turned down to a pinch, the rhubarb comes off — a whole small evening of cooking runs underneath the joke about the niblick, and she keeps the cooking in. She notices the shilling was going into the gas meter. She notices the nun's teeth. "It takes a dreadful number of toadstools to make you realise that life is not one long mushroom."

I keep going back to the ease she names — "We might have spoken a different language — returned from a far country." Do you think the page needed the whole afternoon written down, or only the line about the milk and the honey and the bread, with the rest arriving because the pen was already moving?
