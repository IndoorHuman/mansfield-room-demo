"Dreamed I was shopping, buying underclothes in Cook's and then in Warnock's. But the dream ended horribly." You do not say how. The shops have names, the dream's ending does not, and then the page turns straight into the day: "Another chill, bloodless day. I got up, but all was difficult."

And underneath that, in the same handwriting and the same hour, the accounting: "I have worked to-day in discomfort — not half enough. I could have written a whole story."

That is one half of the page. The other half is all looking. "Saw for the first time an exquisite little crested bird. Its call is a trill, a shake, marvellously delightful." Then the shyness of it — "never had the courage to stop and eat" — and then, with no transition at all, the people in sleighs and on luges, and "Snow is very blue," which is four words and complete. "The icicles at dawn this morning were the colour of opals — blue lit with fire."

So you were up at dawn on a day when all was difficult, and you had the colour of the icicles.

The last thing on the page is a verdict on a borrowed book — "Really awful stuff! I had better keep this for a sign." A sign of what, you don't finish saying. I wonder whether the sign was for the book or for yourself, and whether the crested bird and the opals belong to the same keeping.
