"I hope this pen works. Yes, it does."

The page begins by testing itself, and the test passes, and only then does the day get named — "The last day in September — immensely cold, a kind of solid cold outside the windows." Cold that has an outside and a shape to it, standing there against the glass.

Then, halfway down, the writing turns and speaks to someone: "Don't read this." And it does not stop. It goes straight on into listening — the train whistle, and now the leaves, the dry leaves, and now the fire.

"— fluttering and breaking."

I keep wondering which of them that line belongs to. The fire, surely, but the leaves are still in the ear when it arrives, and the dash lets both have it. Would you have put the dash there if you had meant only one?
