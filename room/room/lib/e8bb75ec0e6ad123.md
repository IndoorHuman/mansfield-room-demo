"Wrote and sent reviews. Stayed in bed, worked. Had a bath. The day was very lovely." Four things in a row and no seam between any of them, and then "I had to work hard," as if the lovely day were part of the work and not a break in it.

The evening turns to the new story. "In the evening began my new story " A Strange Mistake."" The footnote says it was "Subsequently called The Wrong House" — a title that keeps the wrongness and drops the strangeness. I wonder which of the two came to you first, the mistake or the house.

Then the page stops being a list. "At night I could not sleep. My life in London seems immeasurably far and all like a dream." And the strike runs underneath all of it: "l P.O. strike for letters and telegrams," then "Postal strike: no letters, no wire." Nothing arriving in either direction, and the old letters brought out instead — "Tearing up and sorting the old letters. The feeling that comes — the anguish — the words that fly out into one's breast: My darling! My wife! Oh, what anguish! Oh, will it ever be the same?"

You wrote it down without softening it, and then the last thing on the page is a sound: "Lay awake at night listening to the voices. Two men seemed to sing — a tenor and a baritone: then the drowned began."

Even awake, even there, you were still hearing parts.
