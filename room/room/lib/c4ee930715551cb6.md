"A wonderfully pleasant dream about Paris. All went so well." That is the opening, and the two sentences behind it keep the same weather: "The Doctor and his friends all had the same atmosphere. It was good, kind, quietly happy." You do not describe Paris at all. What you describe is how the people in it were, and then you rank it — "I don't know when I've had a dream more delightful."

Then the turn, made in four words: "But the day has not been delightful." Snow, bitter cold, "my congestion worse than ever." "My lung creaks." "I have done no work." And the naming of the place you were in: "I am in a slough of despond to-day, and like everybody in such an ugly place, I am ugly, I feel ugly."

The page does not stay there, but it does not brighten either. It swears: "To-morrow at all costs (here I swear) I shall write a story. This is my first resolution... in this journal. I dare not break it." And it makes room for one more good thing from outside — "It was a beautiful letter and not to be forgotten." The last line is a question you left open: "But why am I so bad?"

What I keep turning over is the word atmosphere. In the dream it belongs to a group of people, all of them sharing one, and it is the thing that made the dream worth writing down before the snow got a word in. I wonder whether that is a word you would ever hand to a waking room, or whether it only ever arrived in sleep.

Also: the single word "Time" sits on its own line, mid-sentence, splitting "yes-" from "terday."
