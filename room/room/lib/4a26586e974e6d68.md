"Felt ill all day. Feeling of violent confusion in my body and head. I feel more ill now than ever, so it seems." The page opens there and does not soften afterwards.

In the middle of it there is a tea-pot. "J. went out and bought a tea-pot and so on; arranged a game of chess and we started playing." The next sentence begins with But. "But the pains in my back and so on make my prison almost unendurable."

Twice, "and so on" — once for the tea-pot, once for the pains in your back. The same three words carrying the shopping and the hurt, both left as lists too long to finish. I find myself wondering whether the chess game ever got as far as an ending, or whether it stayed set up on the table.

"I manage to get up, to dress, to make a show of getting to the restaurant and back without being discovered. But that is literally all. The rest is rather like being a beetle shut in a book, so shackled that one can do nothing but lie down."

Then: "I have got off the ship again and am swept here and there by the sea." The again is doing a great deal there. And under all of it, standing by itself with nothing following it, one word: "Discouragement".
