"I didn't mind the going, but the coming back was rather awful." The two halves of one road, and only the second one gets called anything. Then the reasons, laid down in a row without much between them: "I was unwell, and tired, and my heart could scarcely beat." And straight after that, with nothing to bridge it — "But we made up a song to keep going."

I keep stopping at that *but*. The rain splashed up to your knees and you were frightened, and there was still a song being made up, in the middle of it, out of nothing, to carry the walk home. I wonder whether it had words, or whether it was only a noise the two of you kept up against the weather.

"L. was nice, very nice, sitting with a piece of string in his hand..." — that is where the entry breaks off. A piece of string, and the sentence stops.

The next morning is its own weather. You got up in the dark to be ready for your little maid and watch the dawn coming, "It wasn't up to much, though." "I am wretched. It is a bright winking day. Oh God, my God, let me work! Wasted! Wasted!" I am not going to put anything soft on top of that. The dawn was watched for and did not deliver, and the day was bright, and the work did not come, and you wrote all three down in four lines.

The day before it, there was the fowl. "One brown fowl refused to leave the garden. Long after it knew there was no gap in the wire-netting it kept on running up and down." And then, deliberately: "I must not forget that, nor how cold it was, nor how the mud coated my thin shoes."

Three things named as not-to-be-forgotten, and one of them is a bird going up and down a fence it already understood.
