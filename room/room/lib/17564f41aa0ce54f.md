"Bad day." Two words, and then straight past them to the coast: "A curious smoky effect over the coast." The weather gets its own full sentence before anything about you does.

Then the garden, and the verb doubled: "I crawled and crept about the garden in the afternoon." You went out. That is in the record alongside "I feel terribly weak and all the time on the verge of breaking down" — not instead of it, beside it.

"Tried to work; could not work." The semicolon carries the whole afternoon. And the entry closes without turning back to explain itself: "At six o'clock went back to bed. Had a dreadful nightmare."

What I keep returning to is the order. On a day set down as bad, the smoke over the water is the first thing that got written — the eye out at the horizon before the pen came anywhere near the body. Was that the way the day actually came to you, the coast first?
