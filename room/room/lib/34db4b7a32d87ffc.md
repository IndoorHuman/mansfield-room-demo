"It is time I started a new journal."

And then, immediately, the invitation: "Come, my unseen, my unknown, let us talk together." You open the book by addressing someone who is not there, and the someone answers back before the page is out — "' But it all goes deeper.' Yes, you are right." The quotation marks are yours. You give the other voice its own punctuation.

The accounting is unsparing. "I have been idle; I have failed. Why?" "I read rubbish." "Out of hand? Yes, that describes it — dissipated, vague, not positive." You do not soften any of it, and you do not explain it away either; the reasons are listed — the sun-treatment, the post, the tiredness at night — and then set aside as insufficient.

What stays with me is the image you chose for it. Not a locked door, not an empty desk. "I hear and I acknowledge them, and still I go on sitting at the window, playing with the ball of wool." The stories "wait for me, grow tired, wilt, fade, because I will not come." I wonder whether the wool was actually in your hands that morning, or whether it only arrived once you went looking for the truest picture of sitting still.

And twice in the same page you look outward and say what you see. First: "I look at the mountains and I see nothing but mountains." Then, at the end, after the resolve, after "just going on" — "And especially to keep in touch with Life — with the sky and this moon, these stars, these cold, candid peaks."

Same mountains. Two different pairs of eyes, one page apart.

"I must try and write simply, fully, freely, from my heart."
