"It seemed that the happiness of J. and of me reached its zenith on that day. We could not have been happier; that was the feeling." The page says it twice over, once as statement and once as report — that was the feeling — as though the writing already knew a feeling is a thing you have to attest to.

Then it goes small very fast. The balcony of the bed-room. The sleigh "through masses of heaped-up snow." "He looked so beautiful, too — hatless, strolling about with his hands in his pockets." And straight after that: "He weighed himself. 10 stone." Nothing joins those two sentences. The beautiful one and the number sit flush against each other, and the number is not explained.

"There was a harmonium in the waiting-room." I keep stopping there. It is a whole sentence with no one in it. I wonder whether anybody touched it, or whether it was only furniture in a room where people wait.

The leaving is one clause: "Then I went away, after a quick but not hurried kiss...." The care is all in *not hurried*, and then the four dots.

After that the page turns toward the window and stays there. "It was very beautiful on the way to Sierre. Then I kept wondering if I was seeing it all for the last time — the snowy bushes, the leafless trees." The wondering is not resolved and does not try to be. It is put down in the same voice as the harmonium and the 10 stone, and the last thing on the page is smaller still, and in quotation marks, as if someone else said it, or as if she wanted to hold it at arm's length to look at it:

" I miss the buns."
