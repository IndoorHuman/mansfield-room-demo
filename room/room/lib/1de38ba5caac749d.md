"I simply cannot believe that there was a time when I cared about Turgeniev. Such a poseur! Such a hypocrite!" — and then, two lines on, without any bridge at all: "It's true he was wonderally talented"— no. "It's true he was wonderfully talented but I keep thinking what a good (cinema play?) On the Eve would make."

The verdict comes first and the concession comes second, and the concession is not really a retreat. You do not take back the poseur. You keep him and put him to work.

What holds me is the parenthesis. "(cinema play?)" — the question mark tucked inside the brackets, as though the form were the only thing you were unsure of, having already settled that the thing should be made at all. It is the one place in the entry where you slow down.

I wonder what it is about On the Eve in particular that survived the demotion — whether it is a scene you can see, or only the shape of it.
