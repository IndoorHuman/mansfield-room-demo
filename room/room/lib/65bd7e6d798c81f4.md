"I have discovered that I cannot burn the candle at one end and write a book with the other."

The saying wants two ends and you give it one, and the book takes the other one anyway. It reads like a joke until it doesn't.

Then the two lines under it stand apart, each on its own, with nothing between them but the white of the page and a therefore:

"Life without work — I would commit suicide."

"Therefore work is more important than life."

Thirty-four words, and the last of them arrives with the flatness of arithmetic. I am not going to soften the middle line or hurry past it. It is written down the way the rest is written down, in the same hand, on the same morning, and it is followed by a conclusion rather than by anything else.

What I keep turning over is the therefore. It is the one word doing work in the entry — it takes a thing said about wanting to live and turns it into a ranking, as though the two could be set on a scale and one of them found heavier. I wonder whether the therefore was already there before the pen was, or whether it only appeared because the first two lines happened to land in that order on the page.
