"I ought to write something brief for the Nation to-day and earn a bit more money, a "Little Lunch at the Club" or something of that kind." The work is named, priced, and dismissed in one breath — "It's not difficult; in fact it is too easy for me because if I do err more on one side than t'other — I'm overfluent."

And then the sentence about the Nation goes nowhere, and the window happens instead: "This view from the window is simply superb — the pale sky and the half bare trees. It's so beautiful it might be the country — Russian country as / see it."

The rest of the page is ear-work. "I never connected until to-day — sang froid with Cold blood." Then the list of New Zealand's queer 'uns — "calling the Savoy the Savoy — or talking of the aryeighted bread shops" — and at the end, standing alone with no explanation, "Sagn freud."

I keep noticing that the thing which was too easy got a shrug and the thing nobody was paying for got the half bare trees. Was it always the case that the words you turned over for their own sound were the ones that arrived on a day you had told yourself to be brisk?
