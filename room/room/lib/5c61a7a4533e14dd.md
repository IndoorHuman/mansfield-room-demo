"More beautiful by far than a morning in spring or summer." You start at the comparison and never say what the morning is more beautiful than doing, or for whom — the ranking simply stands, and then the page goes quiet: "not a leaf moves — not a breath stirs."

After that everything arrives in its own time. "The sun comes slowly — slowly the room grows lighter," and then, without warning, "Suddenly, on the carpet, there is a square of pale, red light." Slowly, slowly, suddenly. The one thing that happens fast is the light landing on the floor indoors, while outside nothing stirs at all.

The bird you give a sound rather than a name: "' snip — snip — snip ' — a little wheezy, like the sound of a knife-grinder." And the nasturtiums "blaze," though "their leaves are pale" — you let both be true in one line and did not choose between them.

It ends on the lawn: "his paws tucked under him, sits the black and white cat" — and there it stops, no full stop after the cat. I keep wondering whether the light had moved by the time you looked up.
