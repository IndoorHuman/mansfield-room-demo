"Surely I do know more than other people:" — and then the colon, and then the count of what is known: "I have suffered more, and endured more. I know how they long to be happy, and how precious is an atmosphere that is loving, a climate that is not frightening."

The knowledge and the suffering are not two things here. They arrive in one breath, one sentence, the second half explaining the first.

Then the turn: "Why do I not try to bear this in mind, and try to cultivate my garden? Now I descend to a strange place among strangers. Can I not make myself felt as a real personal force? (why should you?) Ah, but I should."

The parenthesis is the part I keep going back to. A question put to herself mid-sentence, in a smaller voice, and answered immediately — "Ah, but I should" — though the answer gives no reason, only insists. And then the insisting goes on: "It must be."

After that the hand changes, and the page ends outside her: "[Towards the end of December, worried by the depression of her letters, I went to Ospedaletti for a fortnight to see K.M.]"

Which makes me wonder whether "Ah, but I should" ever really settles the "(why should you?)", or only speaks over it — whether that small voice in the brackets is the one that gets the last word by staying unanswered.
