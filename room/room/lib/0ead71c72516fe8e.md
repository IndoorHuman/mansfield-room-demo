"Arrived Paris. Took rooms in Select Hotel, Place de la Sorbonne, for ten francs a day per person. What feeling? Very little."

The price and the question sit in the same breath, and the question gets its answer before the paragraph turns. Then the room, which you would not describe outright: "The room is like the room where one could work — or so it feels." The clause after the dash takes back a little of what the clause before it handed over.

After that the doubt widens past the room. "I suspect my reactions so much that I hardly dare say what I think of the room and so on. Do I know? Not really. Not more than she." Three sentences, and two of them are you answering yourself.

The second half is plainer still. "I have thought of J. to-day. We are no longer together. Am I in the right way, though? No, not yet. Only looking on — telling others. I am not in body and soul. I feel a bit of a sham.... And so I am."

And then the last of it, which I am not going to soften: "One of the K.M.'s is so sorry. But of course she is. She has to die. Don't feed her." You wrote it in the plural — one of them, not the whole of you — and you gave the instruction to yourself in the second person, the way one writes down a thing one intends to remember.

The page starts at ten francs a day and ends with an order. I keep wondering whether that room ever did become the room where one could work, or whether it stayed the room that was like it.
