"All this! All that I write — all that I am — is on the border of the sea. It's a kind of playing. I want to put all my force behind it, but somehow, I cannot!"

The exclamation at both ends, and the border in the middle. Not in the sea and not away from it — on the border of it.

Then, without a bridge, the ground goes flowery: "Ful gay was al the ground, and queynt, And poudred as men had it peynt With many a fresh and sondry flour That casten up ful good savour." Copied out whole, savour and all, on a day named plainly as "a chill, strange day. I can just get about."

And then the letter that was not posted, where the force does go somewhere, straight and without playing: "Say what you like. But let him know that I am practically a hopeless invalid." Followed by money, and a promise about money, and the four dots.

So three kinds of writing on the one page, and only the middle one is borrowed. I keep wondering whether the flowers came in because they were at hand, or because the ground needed to be gay somewhere that day.
