"Now who is to decide between \"Let it be\" and \"Force it\"?"

You ask it in the middle of a sunset, which is where the question seems to belong tonight. The evening had already been put away — "When I had thought the day folded and sealed, came a burst of heavenly bright petals...." — and then it wasn't, and you sat "behind the window, pricked with rain," and drank "the sky and the whisper."

J. gets one sentence: "he says his steed has plenty of strength, but it is idle and shies at such a journey in prospect." You get the longer one, and it is the one with the picture in it — "if mine does not gallop and dance at free will, I am not riding at all, but just swinging from its tail."

Then: "For example, to-day.... To-night he's all sparks."

I notice the whole argument gets settled by the weather, and not by either of you. The hard thing melted and broke into the smallest fountain, and the horse caught fire on its own. Does it always come in from outside like that, the sparks, or is the window just where you happen to be sitting when they arrive?
