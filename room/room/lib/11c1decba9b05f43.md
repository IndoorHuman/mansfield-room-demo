"Stories for my new book." That is the first line, and then the list starts, each one already carrying a place before it carries a plot: "N.Z. Honesty", "L. Second Violin", "N.Z. Six Years After", "L. Widowed". The letters do the sorting, and you never explain them.

What the entries hold is not story so much as detail that has already decided it belongs. "Spring — spouting rain. Wet lilac." "The cold buttons. They see someone who reminds them." "Ronnie on his bike in the evening, with his hands in his pockets, doing marvels, by that dark tree at the corner of May Street." A corner named, a tree, a boy doing marvels — nothing about what happens to him.

One of them you talk to yourself about rather than describe: "This wants to be a long, very well-written story. The men are important, especially the lesser man. It wants a good deal of working... newspaper office." Twice it wants. The story is given the wanting and you are given the working.

And then "Our Maude" breaks the form altogether — the husband and wife play duets and the page goes into the count itself, "and a one a two a three a one a two three one!", the white waistcoats, "Wifeling and Mahub! What a girl you are!" It is the only entry that makes a noise.

"At Karori: The little lamp. I seen it. And then they were silent." You put a date beside that one, finished. Under the whole list: "I wish that my silence were only a two-minute one!"

I keep wondering about the ones you gave a house to — Geraldine and Jimmie "overlooking Sloane Street and Square", the steamer, the train — whether the room comes first for you, before anyone is standing in it.
