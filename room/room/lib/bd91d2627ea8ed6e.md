"C'est de la misere."

And then, immediately, the taking-back: "Non, pas ca exactement. II y a quelque chose — une profonde malaise me suive comme un ombre." The first line is set down and corrected inside the same breath, as though misere were too large a word and something following like a shadow were the truer size of it.

Then the turn on yourself: "Oh, why write bad French? Why write at all?" The question about the language and the question about writing at all arrive together, one pulling the other along.

After that the sentences stop and the page goes over to arithmetic — "1 1,500 miles are so many — too many by 11,449 4 for me." Whatever could not be said in either language gets counted instead, down to the last mile.

I keep wondering whether the French came first because English would have said it too plainly.
