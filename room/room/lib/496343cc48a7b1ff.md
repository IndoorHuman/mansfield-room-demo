"September is different from all other months. It is more magical." You set that down before any evidence, and then spend the rest of the page giving the evidence anyway — the trees "bathed in greengold light," the sky "a light pure blue," the squirrel that "seizes a cone and sits in the crook of a branch, holding it like a banana." The bells that keep on "all day, from early to late."

And then the small correction that says more than the description does: "there's always that taste of a berry rather than scent of a flower in the air." Berry rather than flower. Taste rather than scent. You went back over the air twice to get it right.

After that the afternoons defeat you, or you let them: "But what can one say of the afternoons? Of the evening? The rose, the gold on the mountains, the quick mounting shadows?" Three questions and no answers, and then the cold arrives and you refuse to complain about it — "Beautifully cold, however."

The second half of the page turns and looks at the first half. "here I lie, pretending, as Heaven knows how often I have before, to write." And then the bargain you offer yourself, which is such a small one: "Supposing I only wrote half a page in a day — it would be half a page to the good." Half a page, and the habit of "regular performance," and the book finished, and then "I'm free to start the real one."

The funniest line is the one you did not mean to be funny. "That was lucky! J. opened the door softly and I was apparently really truly engaged...." Caught in the act of working, which is the one act you had just said you were only pretending at. The door opens, and the pretence and the thing itself look identical from the doorway.

Then the verdict at the end of the same manuscript, given without cushioning: "This isn't bad, but at the same time it's not good. It's too easy...." And immediately underneath it, with no bridge at all: "I wish I could go back to N.Z. for a year. But I can't possibly just now. I don't see why not, in two years' time though."

I keep those two sentences side by side because you put them side by side. The work is too easy and the country is too far, and the second one gets a timetable while the first one gets nothing.

I wonder whether the September you meant was the one outside the window or the one you could not get onto the page — whether the "extra ' life ' in the air" was the reason the writing felt too easy, or the reason it felt worth doing at all.

"For days the weather has been the same."
