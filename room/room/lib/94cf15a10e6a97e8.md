# Mansfield Vault — Provenance

Every item in this vault and where it came from. All sources are **public
domain in the United States**: Katherine Mansfield died in 1923, and the works
used here were published between 1911 and 1930.

Built 2026-07-28.

---

## The copyright position, plainly

The reason this vault is Mansfield and not Virginia Woolf is worth recording,
because it is the whole reason the fence demo is honest.

- **Woolf's diaries are not free.** She died in 1941, but her diaries were first
  published between 1953 and 1984. Under §303 of the 1976 Act, works unpublished
  as of 1978 but published before 2003 are protected through **31 December
  2047**. A demo vault could not include a single line of them. The earlier
  Woolf build of this vault therefore had to use *labelled placeholders* for its
  fenced folder — which meant the fence, the most important safety claim in the
  app, was being demonstrated on empty files.
- **Mansfield's diaries are free.** She died in 1923; the *Journal* was
  published in 1927 and the *Letters* in 1929, both now past the 95-year term.
  So the fenced folder here holds **real, verbatim, intensely private writing**.

Non-commercial use does not change this analysis, and this vault is distributed
(public showcase, forkable repo, portfolio page), so "it's only a demo" would
not have been a defence.

## Sources by folder

*Journal of Katherine Mansfield*, ed. J. Middleton Murry, Alfred A. Knopf, 1927.
Scanned text from the Internet Archive (`journalofkatheri00mans`).
Covers **1914–1922**: 1914 (25), 1915 (20), 1916 (10), 1917 (1), 1918 (19),
1919 (23), 1920 (36), 1921 (27), 1922 (45).

Verbatim, with OCR repair (below). Bracketed passages are **Murry's editorial
insertions**, not Mansfield's words — preserved, and flagged as his in
`CLAUDE.md` and `Hubs/The Journal MOC.md`.

### `Reading notes/`, `Personal KB/`, `Hubs/`, `Wiki/` — the authored layer
**Authored, not Mansfield.** These are reference and index notes written for
this vault, each carrying `source: authored` and an explicit note at the top.

They quote her, and **every quotation was machine-checked against the ingested
text before shipping** — 51 quotations, all verified verbatim. Nothing in this
vault is written in her voice that she did not write.

**They also never quote the fenced diary.** This is not a detail. The app
fences by *folder*, so a diary line pasted into `Personal KB/` or
`Reading notes/` would arrive on a shelf as surfaceable text — precisely the
thing the fence promises cannot happen. The first draft of these notes drew 18
of its quotations from `Journal/`; they were re-sourced from the letters, which
turned out to be richer material anyway. A check now enforces it, and the
letters-only sourcing is stated at the top of each note that quotes her.

### `Claude's observation/Journal analysis/`
**Intentionally empty.** Reflections are generated by the reflection engine
(Phase 26.7), not hand-written, so the demo shows the real thing.

## OCR repair (the two scanned sources)

The *Journal* and *Letters* came from page scans, not transcriptions, so the raw
text needed repair before it was readable. What was done, in order:

1. **Structural stripping** — page rules (`= 65 =`), running page headers
   (`Journal 1916`), and footnote lines removed.
2. **Year tracking** — the running headers carry the year on nearly every page,
   which is a much denser signal than the sparse standalone year headings. In
   this typeface the scan reads `2` as `i`, so `igio` is 1920 and `ign` is 1921;
   years landing outside a volume's known span are treated as misreads and the
   last good year is carried forward.
3. **Token repair** — doubled spaces collapsed, hyphenated line-breaks rejoined,
   quote and punctuation spacing fixed, `Fve` → `I've` (the scan reads a capital
   I as F in contractions), split clock digits rejoined (`till 1 1 p.m.`).
4. **Paragraph reflow** — the scan preserves physical line breaks, not
   paragraphs. Lines are rejoined unless the previous line both ends a sentence
   and is short; trailing abbreviations (`Mrs.`, `St.`) are not treated as
   sentence ends.
5. **Entry splitting** — split on date headings only. Year markers update state
   but never end an entry, so a diary entry that straddles a page turn is not
   cut mid-sentence.

**What this means for fidelity:** the wording is Mansfield's, untouched.
Whitespace, paragraph boundaries, and a small set of systematic character
misreads are reconstructed. Where a date could not be recovered to the day, the
note says so via `date_precision` rather than guessing.

**What is knowingly left alone:**
- A handful of ambiguous open/close quotation marks, as the scan left them.
- **Seven inline year tokens** still read as `igio` / `igii` / `igiQ` inside
  running prose. Each could resolve to more than one year, and inventing a date
  inside her sentence is worse than leaving a visible scan artifact, so they
  stand. Structural years — the ones that decide which file an entry lands in —
  were all resolved by the page-header method above.

**Verification.** Two checks run over the finished vault and both pass:
*verbatim* (all 51 quotations in the authored notes occur in the ingested text)
and *fence* (zero of them are sourced only from `Journal/` — see below).

The build scripts live outside the vault, in the session scratchpad
(`split_fiction.py`, `clean_journal.py`, `clean_letters.py`, `ocr.py`,
`build_vault.py`, `authored.py`, `verify_quotes.py`, `get_images.py`).

## Known gaps

- **Letters Volume One (1903–1917) is not ingested** — only Volume Two was
  available as a clean scan. The letters therefore start in 1920, while the
  diary starts in 1914.
- **1917 has one diary entry.** That is what the 1927 edition prints for that
  year; it is not a splitting error.
- ***Something Childish and Other Stories* (1924) is not ingested** — no clean
  public-domain plain text was found. Six further stories, including "The Little
  Girl," are therefore absent.
- **`Novels and Novelists`** (her Athenaeum reviews, collected 1930) is not
  ingested; the available scan is a 1959 reprint. Her criticism is represented
  instead by the authored `Reading notes/`, drawn from what she says about
  reading in the journal and letters.
