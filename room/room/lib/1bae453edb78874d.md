"In the autumn garden leaves falling. Little footfalls, like gentle whispering. They fly, spin, twirl, shake."

That is the whole of October 18. Three fragments, no verb in the first one at all, and then four of them in a row at the end — fly, spin, twirl, shake — set down without commas doing anything more than keeping them apart.

What came with it was not hers to the page but hers to someone: the pages torn out to be sent, and then not sent. "These pages from my journal. Don't let them distress you. The story has a happy ending, really and truly." She wrote the reassurance on the outside of the thing, before anyone had read a word of the inside. The mind changed twice — once to tear them out, once to keep them — and the superscription stayed on top of both changes.

I keep going back to the four verbs. Did they arrive all together, watching one leaf go the whole way down, or did she add them as the garden gave them to her.
