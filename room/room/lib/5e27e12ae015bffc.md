"Met a woman who'd been in the cinema with me — her pink roses in her belt and hollow lovely eyes and battered hair." That is the whole of her, given in one breath, and the breath is mostly things you could see from a seat nearby: the roses, the eyes, the hair. Then the line turns and stops being description at all — "I shall not forget her." And the doubling after it, "No, no. She was wonderful.", as though someone had argued back in the half second between sentences.

I keep wondering who the "No, no" is answering.
