"J. and I ' typed.' I hate dictating; but the story still seems to me to be good. Is it?" The question mark comes at the end of a sentence that had already decided, and you let it stand anyway.

The day itself you gave one line: "A vile cold day." Then the parcel, then the comparison you did not finish — "But when one compares it with A.'s exquisite coat...." — the dots doing the rest.

What sits at the back of it all is not the cold. "All the whole time at the back of my mind slumbers not nor sleeps the idea of Paris, and I begin to plan" — and out of the planning comes the baby named Anton, K. for a godfather, Mme. Tchehov for a godmother. "Such is my dream."

And then the reason the day held: "I don't feel so sinful this day as I did, because I have written something and the tide is still high. The ancient landmarks are covered." I keep wondering whether the tide was the reason for the Paris planning or the other way round — whether the writing came first and let the dream in, or the dream let the writing.

"Let me write better, more deeply, more largely."
