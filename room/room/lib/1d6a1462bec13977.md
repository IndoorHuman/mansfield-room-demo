"A load of wood. Sent review. Cold day." Three flat facts and then the day begins.

The visit gets a line of its own — "Miss S. called — deadly dull. Her yawn and recovery." — and the last paragraph runs everything together without pausing between kinds of thing: "Storm of wind and rain. I had nightmare about J. He and I separated/ Miss S. talked about tulips, but she makes all sound so fussy: the threads of her soul all ravelled."

The nightmare gets no more than the storm does. It goes down between the weather and the tulips and is not returned to. What the slash was doing there, in the middle of "He and I separated/ Miss S. talked about tulips" — whether the pen was hurrying or stopping — is not something the page says.

The wood at the top and the ravelled threads at the bottom, and it is the ravelling you found the words for.
