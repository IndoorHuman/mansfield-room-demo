"A man outside is breaking stones. The day is utterly quiet." The chopping keeps going all the way through — "The old man chops, chops, as though it were a heart beating out there" — and then it is the dog at the mill howling, and a man far away playing the bugle, and the page is full of sound made by people and animals you cannot see.

Then the sewing. "It is so funny to sit quietly sewing, while my heart is never for a moment still." The same beat as the stones, moved indoors.

"I have read and sewed to-day, but not written a word. I want to to-night."

And the plain end of it: "This sad place is killing me. I live upon old made-up dreams; but they do not deceive either of us." Nothing after that, nothing to soften it, and the storm and the untidy room with the newspapers and faded mistletoe sitting just above it — "I hardly slept at all, but it was nice."

I keep wondering who the other one is in "either of us."
