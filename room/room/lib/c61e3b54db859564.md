# On writing

> Authored reading note gathering what she said about her own craft. All
> quotations **verbatim** from the 1929 *Letters* in this vault; nothing here
> is drawn from the fenced diary.

## What art is a proof of
> "It seems to me that very feeling of inevitability that there is in a great
> work of art — is a proof — a profession of faith on the part of the artist
> that this life is not all."

> "But one is only really living when one acknowledges both — or so it seems to
> me — and great art is achieved when the relation between these two is
> perfected."

## The artist and their own moment
> "The question of the Artist and his Time is, I am sure, the Question of
> Questions."

> "The artist who denies his Time, who turns away from it even so much as the
> fraction of a hair is false."

## And art is still not the highest thing
> "Not that I place Art higher than Love or Life."

## On being a worker
> "How I love to think of us as workers, writers — two creatures given over to
> Art."

> "Oh, how pure artists art — how clean and faithful."

## On the years running out
> "But isn't it grim to be reviewing Benson when one might be writing one's own
> stories which one will never have time to write, on the best showing!"

## On accepting the material
> "How marvellous life is, if only one gives oneself up to it! It seems to me
> that the secret of life is to accept life. Question it as much as you like
> after, but first accept it."

> "It's only by risking losing yourself, giving yourself up to Life, that you
> can ever find out the answer."

## To another artist, about his work
> "You're building for the high seas, and Once you do take her out nothing will
> stop her." *(to Richard Murry, a painter, June 1921)*
