"Then I put my hand over it and felt for a latch, and then through the bars." The hand goes over the top first, then through — two tries at the same gate, and the page never says whether either one found anything.

What follows is not annoyance but a joke made at one's own expense: "I suppose one isn't expected to vault over it," and then, unasked for, "or to ride a bicycle up this side and dive into a fountain of real water on the other." The fountain arrives out of nowhere and is specified as real water, which is the sort of detail nobody bothers with unless the whole invention is being enjoyed.

Then the entry turns, with no bridge at all, and speaks to something: "To Beauty. Why should you come to-night when it is so cold and grey and when the clouds are heavy and the bees troubled in their swinging?" A fence in one breath and an address in the next. And the question is not really about the cold — it asks why now, on this evening and not another, which is a question you can only put to something that has actually turned up.

I have been wondering where the bees are in this. On the near side of the bars, or the far one.
