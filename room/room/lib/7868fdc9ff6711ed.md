"I must again write the word" — and then the word gets its own line, in capitals, with nothing to lean on:

DISCIPLINE

and under that

WHICH DO YOU PREFER?

The question is put to yourself and you do not answer it on the page. What follows instead is a plan — "And from day to day after this keep a strict account of what it is that I fail in" — and then, a few lines later, someone speaking to you kindly in your own voice: "Now, Katherine, here goes for to-morrow — Keep it up, my girl."

Then the whole apparatus drops away. "April srd. A good day." Four words, and the account of failures is not kept, and the capitals are not repeated.

What comes after that is the part I keep rereading. A man wakes, does not move, and inside one long moment he has bathed, dressed, crossed the harbour and is waving. A sailor throws the rope. "Beautifully done...." And the whole vision is "so clear and bright and tiny" — a bubble, watched by a baby. He says "I'm there — I'm there. Why do I have to start and do it all so slowly all over again?" and then the moving of his own body pops it, and he forgets it, and sits up smiling, "pulling down his pyjama sleeves."

So the page holds both: the strict account demanded in the evening, and the next morning where nothing is accounted for and a small bright thing arrives whole and complete before the slow version has to be lived through.

I wonder whether the vision came because the ledger was set down, or whether it would have come anyway.
