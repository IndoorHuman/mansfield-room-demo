"Dear Friend. I like your criticism. It is right you should have hated those things in me. For I was careless and false. I was not true in those days." That is how the page starts, and it does not stay there long. Two lines on and the same hand is saying "But I have been trying for a long time now to " squeeze the slave out of my soul."... I just want to let you know" — the letter closing its own subject before anyone can answer it.

Then, without a seam: "Oh, I am in the middle of a nice story [The Garden Party]. I wish you would like it. I am writing it in this exercise book, and just broke off for a minute to write to you." The apology and the exercise book share a minute. The breaking-off is mentioned as though the reader can see the pen lifted.

And the weather gets in on its own terms — "It's a sunny, windy day — beautiful. There is a soft roaring in the trees and little birds fly up into the air just for the fun of being tossed about."

At the end of the manuscript the tone drops to a flat measure: "This is a moderately successful story, and that's all." "It's somehow, in the episode at the lane, scamped." One word, scamped, doing all the work of the complaint.

And then the rain page, which is not measured at all. "It is late night, very dark, very still. Not a star to be seen. And now it has come on to rain. What happiness it is to listen to rain at night; joyful relief, ease; a lapping-round and hushing and brooding tenderness, all are mingled together in the sound of the fastfalling rain." The steps come up the stone staircase, Ernestine at the door, and the answer to it is "Let me weep too." Under that, in a different key entirely: "You ought to keep this, my girl, just as a warning to show what an arch-wallower you can be."

I keep turning over whether the last line is the same voice as the rain or a second one that arrived later to stand over it — and whether the one who wrote "scamped" and the one who wrote "joyful relief, ease" ever agree about anything on the same day.

"There is a soft roaring in the trees and little birds fly up into the air just for the fun of being tossed about."
