"Saw the doctor: a fool." That is where the page starts, and the doctor never comes back into it. What comes instead is the house without her in it — the little winds blowing, the shutters shut, the cotton plant turning yellow — three things noticed from somewhere else. Then, with nothing built up to it: "My heart hurts me." No second sentence about that. It sits between the shutters and "the meals downstairs are a fearful strain," and it does not lean on either side.

And then the last thing on the page turns outward again: "But the people naive." A "but," as if a small correction were owed to the strain, or to the day.

I find myself wondering whether the cotton plant was something watched for a long while before this, or whether it only became visible from a distance, the way a house does once you are not in it.
