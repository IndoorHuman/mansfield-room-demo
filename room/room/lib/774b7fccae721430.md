"We go to Cornwall to-morrow, I suppose."

That is the whole first paragraph, and the "I suppose" is doing more work than the going is. Then, with a line of white space between, everything else arrives at once: "I've re-read my diary. Tell me, Is there a God? I'm old to-night. Ah, I wish I had someone to love me, comfort me and stop me thinking."

Four sentences, and each one a different size. The re-reading is reported flatly. The question is put to no one in particular — "Tell me" — and nobody in the entry is there to be told. And the last one does not ask for company alone; it asks for company that would "stop me thinking," which is a stranger and more specific wish than being less alone.

Underneath sits the bracket, in the other voice, doing the plain arithmetic of where a body went: "After two changes of rooms in Chelsea, and a fortnight in a furnished cottage at Merryn in Cornwall, we took in September 1914 an ugly cottage at The Lee, near Missenden in Buckinghamshire." So the Cornwall of "I suppose" did happen — a fortnight of it — and then somewhere else, and the cottage got called ugly by someone who was not going to soften it.

I keep circling the fact that the night of the questions and the fortnight in Cornwall are stacked on the same page, one in her hand and one in a bracket. Does an entry like this one read differently to you when you know, from the note under it, where she was standing a month later?
