The daughter of the watch-smith arrives first, and then everything about her arrives at once — "Her weak heart, queer face, queer voice, awful clothes" — with no verb anywhere to slow the list down. A few lines later the father is back and he has changed trade: "The father was fair and youthful to look at. He was a clock-maker." Watch-smith at the top of the page, clock-maker at the bottom, and nothing in between that corrects either one.

Edie's Sunday hat is "trimmed beyond words," and then you go on and find words for a great many other things — the pools and rivers "quicksilver," the orchard where "apples flashed like stars," figs that "tasted of wine and honey."

"Oh, that tree at the corner of May Street" — and then, straight after, "I forgot it until this moment." The forgetting is put down beside the tree as though it belonged to the description of it.

The name gets spoken three times on the page: "A-vig... Avig... Avig-non." I keep wondering whether those dots are how she said it or how you heard it afterwards, sitting down to write, still hearing her try.
