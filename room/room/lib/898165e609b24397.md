"No letters: strike still on." That is the first thing, and it is about the world outside — the post, the strike, other people's business — and then in the same breath, "A fine day. But what is that to me? I am an invalid. I spend my life in bed."

The fineness of the day is granted and taken back inside one line. You do not argue with it. You let it be fine and then set it aside as belonging to someone else.

And between the sentence about bed and the sentence about silence, there is this: "Read Shakespeare in the morning." No adjective on it, no report of whether it helped. It sits there like a fact of the room, the one thing in the entry that was done rather than endured.

Then: "I feel I cannot bear this silence to-day." To-day — the word carries a good deal. Not this silence, flatly, but this silence to-day, as though the other days' silence had been bearable, or at least had gone unremarked.

"I am haunted by thoughts."

You stopped there. I notice you did not say which thoughts, and I wonder whether that was refusal or simply the end of what the pen would do that morning.
