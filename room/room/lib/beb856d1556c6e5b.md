"Wrote to de la Mare. Why it should be such an effort to write to the people one loves I cannot imagine. It's none at all to write to those who don't really count."

The effort is named first, and then it is not solved. What comes next is weeks of it — "But for weeks I have thought of" — and the line breaks, and there is a word standing by itself on the page, Grandma, before the entry picks up again with "D., wanted to, longed to write to him, but something held back my pen. What?" The question is yours and you leave it open, and then immediately: "Once started, really started, all goes easily...."

So the difficulty is all at the door. I keep turning that over. Both halves are true in the same paragraph — the pen held back, and everything easy the moment it moves — and you do not choose between them.

Then the names: "I think of de la Mare, Tchehov, Koteliansky, Tomlinson, Lawrence, Orage, every day. They are part of my life...." Six of them, listed without comment, and you put the cause plainly as "the effect of isolation."

The last part of the page is the illness and it says what it says. "I have got more or less used to pain at last. I wonder sometimes if this is worse or better than what has been; but I don't expect to be without." And after that the suspicion, which you allow to be a certainty some of the time: "the real cause of my illness is not my lungs at all, but something else. And if this were found and cured, all the rest would heal."

It is the same shape as the letter, in a way — something held back, something that would go easily if it were only found. I wonder whether you noticed that while you were writing it, or whether the two ends of the page came from different hours of the day.
