"My fits of temper are really terrifying." It is the first line and it does not build to anything — the page is torn, the head is lost, and then J. is in the room asking "What is the matter? What have you done? Why? You look all dark." He draws the curtains back and calls it an effect of light. The studio mirror does not go along with him: "I was a deep earthy colour, with pinched eyes."

And then, with no bridge at all, the weather. "It is a dark reluctant day. The fire makes a noise like a flag — and there is the familiar sound from below of someone filling buckets." Stiff, unused to writing, and still the table has someone sitting opposite it.

The room gets its own defence: "My room really has for me a touch of fairy. Is there anything better than my room? Anything outside?" The kitten says not, though the case is admittedly biased — the sun throws the shape of the window onto the carpet and "in those four little square fields the silly flies wander, ever so spied upon by the little lion under the sommier frill."

The question that comes next is not answered either: "Oh dear — Oh dear — where are my people? With whom have I been happiest? With nobody in particular. It has all been mush of a mushness."

Then one word: "Later." The little lion took sick and was taken away, and the writing does not soften any part of it — two weeks in great torture, the ball bought for it, the standing up on hind legs to try to mew with no sound coming. "I never saw anything more pitiful." Nothing follows it on the page; nothing is made of it.

I keep looking at that "Later." and wondering how much of an evening or a season it is holding, whether the sentence about the four little square fields was still true when it was written — the page gives no way to know.

"Others leave me — all things leave me, You remain."
