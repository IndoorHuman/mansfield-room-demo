"Since leaving Montana I have written about a page." That is the count you gave, and the sentence after it does not defend the page — it goes straight to "The rest of the time I seem to have slept! This, of course, started all the Old Fears: that I should never write again, that I was getting sleeping sickness and so on." The Old Fears take capitals. They arrive already named, already known, nothing needing introduction.

Then the same evening turns and says: "perhaps a time of convalescence was absolutely necessary. The mind was choked with the wrack of all those dreadful tides."

And then the swallows, which have nothing to do with any of it. "their flutter-flutter, their velvetforked tails, their transparent wings that are like the fins of fishes. The little dark head and breast golden in the light." After that the garden, and the raked paths, and "Then, the silence."

You order the book destroyed — "Tear this book up! Tear it up, now!" — and the book is here, and the next line is a plan: "I should like to write the canary story to-morrow."

The longest, most patient looking on the page goes to the love-birds. Green underbody, wings mole tipped with yellow, "a touch of pale-blue on the chops and on the top of the beak," the female "yellow with overbody of pale green in deli- cate pencil lines." Feather by feather, and no argument anywhere in it.

"But better far write twaddle or anything, anything, than nothing at all."

I find myself wondering whether the to-morrow the canary story was promised is the one it actually got.
