"L.M. and I travelled miles to-day. We sat in a bus talking, and now and again when I looked up, I kept seeing the squares with their butterfly leaves just ready to fly."

The looking up is the whole of it. The talk goes on underneath, uninterrupted, and the squares arrive only in the gaps — leaves not flying, just ready to fly, which is a different and much finer thing to catch.

Then the page narrows to two people and their small ceremonies: "the old haunts — Queen Anne Street" and "one of the little lanes and short cuts that we know so well — side by side, talking." And in the middle of the walking, a full stop for something tiny: " Let me tie your veil," and I stop; she ties it and we walk on again.

The Persian shop holds still. A red and black silk curtain, a black hat that looked enormous, and " these things — feel how soft they are," spoken just above her breath for tiredness.

I keep wondering whether it is the softness she is being offered or the offering itself that got written down.
