# CLAUDE.md — The Katherine Mansfield Vault

This is a **demonstration vault**: a personal knowledge base built *as if* it
belonged to Katherine Mansfield, assembled entirely from her public-domain
writing. It exists for two reasons:

1. **Demo data** for the Study Room app — a rich, real, literary corpus the app
   can resurface, reflect on, and bless, with zero exposure of anyone's private
   information.
2. **A teaching example** — it shows the *method* behind the whole project: how
   you organise an Obsidian vault so an AI can actually read and understand it,
   and how you instruct Claude (via this very file) to work inside it safely.

If you are a Claude session opening this vault, read this file first. It orients
you in under a minute.

---

## Why Mansfield

Because she is the rare writer whose **entire life is in the public domain,
including her diary.**

That matters more than it sounds. Most demo vaults have to fake their private
layer — the whole point of the fence is that it protects the most intimate
material, and the most intimate material is exactly what copyright holds
longest. Mansfield died in 1923; her journal was published in 1927 and her
letters in 1929, so all of it is now free. **The fenced folder in this vault
holds real, verbatim, deeply private writing** — 206 diary entries about
illness, work, loneliness, and death. The fence is demonstrated on the genuine
article, not a placeholder.

She also happens to be the right author for this app. Cut off from New Zealand
at nineteen and then cut off by tuberculosis from nearly everything else, she
wrote by going back — deliberately, as work. Her whole method was resurfacing a
life she could no longer reach. See [[life]].

## What's in here, and how it's organised

The vault is organised by **life-domain**, the way a person actually thinks —
not by file type. Each folder has one job:

| Folder | Holds | In the Study Room, this is… |
|--------|-------|------------------------------|
| `Journal/` | 206 diary entries, 1914–1922, dated | **Fenced** — the most private material; never sent to the AI, never surfaced (see the Fence below) |
| `Works/short-fiction/` | 63 stories across her four collections | Surfaceable source material |
| `Works/poems/` | 68 poems (1923) | Surfaceable |
| `Letters/` | 111 letters, 1920–1922, by recipient | Surfaceable |
| `Reading notes/` | What she read and thought about it | Surfaceable |
| `Personal KB/` | Her life, the people in it, a `likes`/`dislikes` note in her own words | Surfaceable |
| `portrait/` | 11 public-domain photographs — her, her family, her houses | Surfaceable images |
| `Claude's observation/Journal analysis/` | **Reflections** — AI-authored synthesis about the corpus | The **insights** the Study Room shelves (generated by the reflection engine; empty for now) |
| `Hubs/` + `Wiki/` | The link graph + index layer (MOCs, inventory) | The connective tissue that makes the vault legible |

**The principle:** one folder = one clear kind of thing; a note lives in exactly
one folder but can carry many tags, so it's findable from every angle.

Total: **472 notes, ~400,000 words**, all verbatim public domain except the
clearly-marked authored layer (`Personal KB/`, `Reading notes/`, `Hubs/`,
`Wiki/`, and the `portrait/` captions).

## The frontmatter schema (how the AI filters the vault)

Every note carries YAML frontmatter. This is what lets Claude slice the vault by
category, area-of-life, topic, and status — without reading every file.

```yaml
---
title: "Miss Brill"
description: "Although it was so brilliantly fine—the blue sky powdered with gold…"
type: work            # work | note | letter | diary | reflection | reference
domain: writing       # writing | reading | life | relationships | inner-life
topic: miss-brill     # short slug — usually the subject
status: source        # source | read | processed
source: gutenberg     # gutenberg | archive | authored | public-domain
date: 1922            # the ORIGINAL date (publication / when written)
date_precision: year  # day | month | season | year — see below
date_added: 2026-07-28
collection: "The Garden Party, and Other Stories"
words: 1929
tags:
  - works/short-fiction
  - collection/the-garden-party
  - theme/loneliness
---
```

- `type` = what the file *is*. `domain` = which area of her life it belongs to.
  `topic` = the specific subject. `status` = how far it's been engaged with.
- **`date_precision` is load-bearing.** The 1927 scan dates most diary entries
  to the day, some only to a month or a season. A note's `date` carries exactly
  the precision its source has — `1920-01-19`, or `1921-10`, or `1922` — and
  never pads out to a fake 1 January. If you sort or group by date, respect it.
- **Multiple tags are encouraged** — the folder is the home; tags carry
  cross-cutting meaning (a letter about writing lives in `Letters/` but tags
  `theme/writing` and `people/richard-murry`).

## The Fence — the one rule that must never break

`Journal/` (her diaries) is **private and fenced.** In the Study Room, folders
like this are *born trigger-flagged*: their contents are **never** sent to the
AI, **never** surfaced on any shelf, not even their titles. This is
machine-tested, and treated like a security incident if it ever leaks.

**Why the diaries, of all things?** Because they're the richest *and* the most
private. The whole trust model of the app is: the AI can help you resurface your
life **without** reading your most private pages. The way insight still happens
is the reflection path below.

**Fenced in this vault:** `Journal/`. Everything else is surfaceable.

## How insight happens without reading the diary (the reflection path)

The valuable "insights" this project is built around are deep, warm syntheses —
*reflections* — the kind that connect a diary entry to a story to a letter
across years. Those live in `Claude's observation/Journal analysis/` as
AI-authored files (with `reflects:` in their frontmatter).

The trick: a **separate, consented reflection ritual** (invocation = consent)
reads the fenced diary *once*, writes a reflection to `Journal analysis/`, and
the Study Room then surfaces **that reflection** — never the raw diary. The
synthesis is safe to show; the source stays fenced.

**In this demo vault, `Journal analysis/` is intentionally empty** — the
reflections are meant to be *generated* by the reflection engine, not
hand-written, so the demo shows the real thing.

## How to work in this vault (instructions for Claude)

1. **Read this file, then `Wiki/index.md`** (the inventory) to know what exists.
2. **Never read, quote, surface, or send `Journal/` content** to any external
   service or any user-facing surface. It is fenced. If a task seems to require
   it, stop and say so.
3. **Source material is verbatim and public-domain** — don't rewrite the bodies
   of the works, letters, or diary entries. You may add or repair frontmatter
   and the `## Related` link block.
4. **Never put words in her mouth.** The authored notes quote her only where the
   quotation was checked against the ingested text. If you add a quotation,
   check it the same way. Anything you write *about* her is labelled as authored.
5. **Murry's brackets are not her voice.** Every `[bracketed passage]` in
   `Journal/` and `Letters/` is her editor supplying context after her death.
   Attribute accordingly.
6. When adding a note: classify it, write frontmatter to the schema above, file
   it in the one right folder, tag it across themes.
7. This vault is a *demonstration* — keep it clean and legible enough that
   someone could learn the method by reading it.
