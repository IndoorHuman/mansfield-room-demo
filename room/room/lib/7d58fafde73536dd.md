"Dreamed of M.S. An important dream; its tone was important." You say it twice over, importance and then tone, as though the second were the correction of the first — and then the gallery over the sea, your "Isn't it beautiful?" and his weary " No doubt." You leave his definition of the two kinds of women to trail off in dots. Whatever it was, it did not get written down.

Then the turn: "But I was not so wicked to-day." The but does a lot of work there. After it comes Cosmic Anatomy, read a good deal of and understood far better, and the plain admission — "Yes, such a book does fascinate me. Why does J. hate it so?" The question sits there unanswered, next to the fascination, not cancelling it.

And then the middle of the page, which is the part I keep going back over: "To get a glimpse of the relation of things — to follow that relation and find it remains true through the ages enlarges my little mind as nothing else do." You give the example — "hot + bun may mean Taurus, Pradhana, substance" — and immediately take it back: "No, that's not really what attracts me; it's that reactions to certain causes and effects always have been the same." You correct yourself mid-thought and keep both halves on the page. And the proof you reach for is your own: "It wasn't for nothing Constantia chose the moon and water, for instance!"

"Read Shakespeare. The snow is thicker, it clings to the branches like white new-born puppies."

Two sentences, and the second one is the whole of the outside. I wonder whether the snow was already doing that while you read, or whether you looked up at the end and found it had.
