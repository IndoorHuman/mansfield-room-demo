"The meals here are a horror. I seem to be sitting hours and hours there, and the people are ugly." Then the turn, with nothing to soften it: "Nevertheless, thank God I am here, in sound of the train, in reach of the post."

The good thing is not the place. It is what can get to you there, and what can leave — the train heard rather than taken, the post that arrives from elsewhere. Both of them are about a somewhere else being still attached.

I keep wondering what the ugliness of the people was made of — whether it was faces at all, or only the hours and hours of sitting.
