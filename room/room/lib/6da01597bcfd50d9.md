"First comes L.M. I give her orders." The whole morning is built that way, one person at a time, each with a line of instruction and a small kindness folded into it: "Be gentle with her: help her to make the beds; and just tell her how everything must be."

What I keep turning over is where the doubt goes. With Ralph it sits inside a bracket — "sending her away (I hope) feeling important and happy" — and then, upstairs, with Maud, there is no bracket at all: "I left her happy. I know I did." The evidence you give for that is not anything you did. It is the tram, and the birds that "sang 'something beautiful,'" and a hill that stopped being "the long drag up the hill." She told you and you believed her.

Downstairs it is flowers, and a woman on her knees polishing and saying it was such a fine day. "We had a little joke or two and I came away." Then back to L.M. for one more moment, the only order that is really a gift: "Keep your energies for something important!"

And only after all of them: "Then I sit down to work, and there comes a steady, pleasant vibration from the ship." I wonder whether the vibration is the ship at all, or whether that is just what a morning sounds like when every person in the house has been handed their part of it and gone off carrying it.

"If only I could always control these four women like this! I must learn to."
