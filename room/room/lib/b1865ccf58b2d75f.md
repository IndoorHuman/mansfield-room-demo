"Love will win if only I can stick to it. It will win after all and through all" — you wrote the winning twice, once with a condition on it and once without, and the second time you did not take the condition back, you just let the sentence go past it.

Before that the day is all arrivals. "Connie and Jinnie came and brought a notice of my book [\"Bliss\"]. I brought in more flowers. Saw the lovely palm." Three short ones in a row, each a thing that came in or was looked at, and then the fourth sentence is not that kind of sentence at all.

I keep wondering which way the sticking runs — whether it is the love you meant to hold on to, or the saying that it will win.
