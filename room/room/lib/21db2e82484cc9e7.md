"When I leave her hands I feel hung with wreaths." That is the line the page turns on, and it comes early — before the pretty rooms, before the silver tankard — as if it had to be set down first.

What follows keeps its ledger honestly. "A silly, unreal evening at Miss R.'s." "A.R. has a pert, nice face — that was all." "I was wretched." And then the sentence that does not soften itself: "I am wretchedly unhappy among everybody — and the silence..."

Between the wreaths and the wretchedness there is one small refusal I keep noticing: "I have nothing to say to l charming ' women. I feel like a cat among tigers." The quotation marks around charming are doing something. So is the cat — not a mouse among tigers, a cat, the wrong animal in the right shape.

And at the top, unattended, the new moon, "6h. 9m. p.m. (I didn't see it, though)." The time recorded to the minute for a thing not looked at. I wonder whether you keep that parenthesis the way I do — the exactness of it, held out beside the admission, neither one cancelling the other.

"Pretty rooms and pretty people, pretty coffee."
