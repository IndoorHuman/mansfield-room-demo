"I have begun two stories, but then I told them and they felt betrayed." Not that you felt betrayed by telling them — the stories did. They are the ones with the injury, and you set it down as a matter of fact about how they behave: "It is absolutely fatal to give way to this temptation...."

And then, on the same page, the same day, you begin one anyway. "To-day I began to write, seriously, The Weak Heart, — a story which fascinates me deeply." What it needs is listed like an ingredient list — "a very subtle variation of ' tense ' from the present to the past and back again — and softness, lightness, and the feeling that all is in bud" — and the listing gets more particular as it goes, until it stops being a list and becomes the Thorndon Baths themselves.

I keep looking at the place where the sentence breaks. "the wet, moist, oozy..." — three words reaching for a fourth, and instead of the fourth word you turn and speak to yourself. Is the interruption impatience, or is it that the thing had already been got and there was no use finishing the reach?

"A bad spell has been on me." That sits at the top, before any of it.
