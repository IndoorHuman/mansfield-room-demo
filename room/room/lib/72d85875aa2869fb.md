"Dreamed I was back in New Zealand." Then, straight after, the getting up, and the day being fine, and the snow going off the trees and the roof — "The drops were not like raindrops, but bigger, softer, more exquisite." You let the dream stand as its own line and did not go back for it.

Then the men on the snowy road, and the pole they were trying to raise, and the lunch out of a paper before any of it began. "It is very beautiful to see people sharing food." That is the whole judgement, no hedging in it. And the one who climbed up: "The one in the tree turned into a kind of bird, as all people do in trees — chuckled, laughed out, peered from among the branches, careless."

The afternoon with M. is set down as flatly as the weather. "We said things we meant; we were sincere, but at the back there was nothing but falsity." You do not soften it and you do not explain it: "No, she is not my friend."

And then, on the same page, the story finished in four or five hours, and this — "There it was, new and complete." I keep wondering whether the not sleeping was the cost of that or part of it.
