"Went for a drive. All the way gay. The house and the girl. Couldn't work: slept again. Dreadful pain in joints. Fearfully noisy house!" — six little reports, one after another, and the pain gets no more room on the page than the drive does.

And then the sentence that does get room: "Saw an orange-tree, an exquisite shape against the sky: when the fruit is ripe the leaves are pale yellow." It is the only thing in the entry with a colon in it, the only thing you stayed with long enough to go on past the first look. The house was still noisy. The tree was still exquisite. Both true in the same handful of lines, neither one arguing with the other.

I keep wondering whether you already knew about the pale yellow leaves, or learned it standing there.
