"I thought and thought this morning but to not much avail." That is where the page starts, and it does not stay there long — two lines later the whole thing turns over: "I am all right — sky-high. And even in my brain, in my head, I can think and act and write wonders — wonders; but the moment I really try to put them down I fail miserably."

What the page does next is not fail. It lists. "Geneva is a long story, and Hamilton is very short, and this ought to be written to my brother really, and another about the life in New Zealand. Then there is Bavaria." And Paris after that, and "God! When shall I write all these things and how?" — a question with a shape to it, five things counted off by someone who knows exactly which is long and which is short.

There is a kitten in here too, wanted at a particular size: "I'd like to have a kitten about as big as a little tea-pot." And a pen worried over in the middle of everything else: "Does this pen write? Oh, I do hope so. For it's really beastly to have a pen that doesn't."

I keep turning over the fact that the morning with no wit in it is the same morning that holds Bavaria and Paris and the tea-pot kitten. Which of those was the thing that came first, do you think — the sky-high, or the sitting down?
