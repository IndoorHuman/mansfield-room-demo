"The woman who does the massage is not really any good." That is the first line, and it is about somebody else's hands, and then the page turns inward without a pause: "My life is queer here. I like my big airy room, but to work is so hard. At the back of my mind I am so wretched."

Four sentences and each one changes subject, and none of them argues with the one before. The room is liked. The work is hard. The wretchedness is at the back, which is a place, and you gave it a place.

Then: "But all the while I am thinking over my philosophy — the defeat of the personal." The "all the while" is doing something — it runs underneath the massage and the airy room and the wretchedness, at the same time as all of them, not after.

I keep wondering what the dash is holding off. Was the phrase already finished in your head before you wrote the first half of the sentence?
